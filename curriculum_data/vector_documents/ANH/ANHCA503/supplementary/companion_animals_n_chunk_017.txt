The Senses
17

Camels have well-developed senses that are adapted for their desert habitat.
Their eyesight is excellent, allowing them to see long distances and detect
predators or other threats. They also have a keen sense of smell, which
helps them locate food and water sources over vast distances. Additionally,
camels possess a third eyelid that protects their eyes from sand and dust
while still allowing them to see.
Locomotion
Camels are built for efficient locomotion across sandy terrains. They have
long legs with large, padded feet that prevent sinking into soft sand. Their
unique gait involves moving both legs on one side of the body followed by
both legs on the other side, which provides stability and balance on uneven
surfaces. This adaptation allows camels to travel long distances with
minimal energy expenditure.
Skin and Hair
The skin of camels is thick and tough, providing protection against harsh
environmental conditions such as sunburn and abrasions from sandstorms.
Their fur is dense and can vary in color from light beige to dark brown; it
reflects sunlight during hot days while providing insulation during cooler
nights. The fur also helps reduce heat absorption by creating a layer of air
between the skin and the external environment.
