Donkeys: Donkey mating rituals involve braying and physical displays from

Camels: Male camels engage in aggressive displays during the mating season to

Territory Marking Territorial marking is crucial for many companion animals
as it helps establish boundaries within their environment.
Dogs: Dogs mark territory primarily through urination and feces. This behavior
communicates information about their presence to other dogs.
Cats: Cats use both urine marking and scratching as forms of territorial
signaling. They have scent glands located on their paws that release
pheromones when they scratch surfaces.
Horses: Horses communicate territory through body language rather than
scent marking; however, they do establish dominance hierarchies within herds
that dictate access to resources like food or mates.
Donkeys: Donkeys use vocalizations along with scent marking to establish
territory within their social groups.
Camels: Camels mark territory using urine and fecal matter but rely more on
visual cues due to their social structure in herds.
Defense Behavior Defense mechanisms vary widely among these species based
on their natural instincts and domestication levels.
Dogs: Dogs may bark or growl as warning signals when they perceive threats.
Some breeds have been specifically bred for protective instincts toward humans
or property.
