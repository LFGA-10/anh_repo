B. Cleaning and Disinfection of Materials, Tools, and Equipment Used
In addition to cleaning the environment itself, it is vital to maintain hygiene for
all materials and equipment used in caring for companion animals:
Personal Protective Equipment (PPE): Caregivers should wear appropriate
PPE (gloves, masks) when handling animals or cleaning potentially
contaminated areas or items.
Tools & Equipment Cleaning: All tools (e.g., grooming brushes, leashes)
should be cleaned after each use to prevent cross-contamination between
animals.
Disinfecting Procedures: Use suitable disinfectants on all equipment
according to guidelines provided by manufacturers or veterinary authorities.
Pay special attention to items that come into direct contact with animals.
Storage Practices: Store cleaned tools in a clean area away from potential
contaminants to maintain their hygiene until next use.
Regular Maintenance Checks: Conduct routine checks on equipment for any
signs of wear or contamination that may necessitate replacement or additional
cleaning efforts.
C. Disposing Wastes
Proper waste disposal is critical in biosecurity practices:
Waste Segregation: Separate waste into categories such as general waste
(non-hazardous), hazardous waste (potentially infectious), and recyclable
materials before disposal.
Biological Waste Disposal: For biological waste (e.g., feces, urine-soaked
bedding), use designated containers that comply with local regulations
regarding hazardous waste management.
