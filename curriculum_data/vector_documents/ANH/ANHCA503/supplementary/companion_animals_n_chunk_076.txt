Dogs: Dogs are omnivores and exhibit scavenging behavior. They often eat
quickly and may show signs of food guarding. Their feeding can be influenced
by social dynamics within a household.

Cats: Cats are obligate carnivores, meaning they require a diet primarily
composed of meat. They tend to hunt small prey, which involves stalking and
pouncing behaviors. Domestic cats may also exhibit “play” behaviors that
mimic hunting.
Horses: Horses are herbivores with a grazing behavior that involves continuous
eating throughout the day. They prefer to eat in groups and will often establish
a pecking order when feeding.
Donkeys: Similar to horses, donkeys are also herbivores but have different
foraging strategies. They tend to be more selective about their food choices and
can exhibit behaviors like nibbling or browsing.
Camels: Camels are also herbivorous but have adapted to arid environments
where they consume tough vegetation. Their feeding behavior includes chewing
cud, which is essential for digesting fibrous plant material.
Sexual Behavior Sexual behaviors among these animals include courtship
rituals, mating practices, and parental care.
Dogs: Female dogs go into heat approximately twice a year, during which males
display courtship behaviors such as marking territory and vocalizing. After
mating, females exhibit nesting behaviors when pregnant.
Cats: Female cats also go into heat several times a year. Males display
territorial marking through scent marking and vocalizations to attract females.
Horses: Stallions engage in elaborate courtship displays that include
vocalizations and physical posturing to attract mares during their estrous
cycle.
