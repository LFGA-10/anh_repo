❖ Hearing
The ear canal of the dog is much deeper than that of people and creates a
better funnel to carry sound to the ear drum. Dogs are also better at
distinguishing the direction of a sound, which is an adaptation useful for
hunting. Unfortunately, this deeper ear canal predisposes dogs to ear
problems. Grease, wax, and moisture can build up in the ear, leading to
inflammation and infection. Floppy ears or hair within the ears further limit
ventilation, making matters worse. This is why many dogs need frequent
preventive ear cleaning.

❖ Smell and taste
Dogs have an extraordinarily acute sense of smell; it is about a million times
more sensitive than that of people.
This is why dogs are able to sniff out drugs and explosives at airports, search
for human victims at disaster sites (including victims deep under water), and
follow the scent track of criminals. Dogs also have an organ on the roof of the
mouth that allows them to taste certain smells. However, dogs gain much more
information about food from smell than from taste.
❖ Locomotion
All 4 of the dog's limbs are maximized for locomotion, from a steady walk to a
rapid sprint. In many respects, dogs run like horses, and have the same 4
gaits: walk, trot, canter, and gallop. Most dogs can swim, although some
breeds specifically developed for swimming (for example, retrievers) can swim
better than others (such as Bulldogs).
