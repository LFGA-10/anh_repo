B. Mating Techniques
Natural Mating Behavior
Natural mating behavior in companion animals such as dogs, cats, horses,
donkeys, and camels involves instinctual actions that facilitate reproduction.
Each species exhibits unique mating rituals and behaviors that are influenced
by their biological and environmental factors.
Dogs: In dogs, the female (bitch) goes into heat (estrus) approximately twice a
year. During this period, she emits pheromones that attract males (dogs). The
male dog will often display courtship behaviors such as sniffing and following
the female. Once the female is receptive, mating occurs through copulation,
which can last from 5 to 30 minutes.
Cats: Feline mating behavior is characterized by vocalizations and physical
displays. Female cats (queens) also go into heat several times a year. They
exhibit behaviors like rolling on the ground and increased affection towards
males. Male cats will engage in aggressive courtship tactics to assert
dominance over other males.
Horses: In horses, the mare’s estrous cycle lasts about 21 days with a heat
period of 5-7 days. Stallions may exhibit strong interest in mares during this
time, displaying behaviors such as nipping or chasing. Natural mating involves
the stallion mounting the mare after a series of courtship behaviors.
