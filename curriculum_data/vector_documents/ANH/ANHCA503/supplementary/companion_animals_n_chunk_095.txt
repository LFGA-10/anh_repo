5. Leading and Walking
Walking with a camel involves establishing leadership by walking slightly
ahead of them—about two meters away—which encourages trust and safety
97

during interactions. This position allows handlers to guide movements
effectively while also reducing risks if the camel becomes startled.
6. Accepting Touch
Finally, once sufficient trust has been built through previous steps, handlers
can introduce touch gradually. It’s essential not to rush this process as
premature physical contact can lead to stress or setbacks in training progress.
Instead, handlers should reward compliance by allowing space after requests
are fulfilled.
Throughout these stages of training, repetition plays an important role in
reinforcing learned behaviors and commands over time as camels age or
change temperament.
By following these structured steps in camel training—establishing trust first,
then gradually introducing equipment like headstalls before teaching
commands—handlers can create a rewarding relationship with their camels
that enhances both safety and enjoyment in handling these remarkable
animals.
3.3: Monitoring of companion animal behavior
Monitoring the behavior of companion animals is essential for understanding
their health and well-being. This process can be broken down into several key
components, including direct observation, comparison to baseline behaviors,
and tracking specific behavioral metrics such as activity level, response to
approach and stimulus, appetite, and elimination.
