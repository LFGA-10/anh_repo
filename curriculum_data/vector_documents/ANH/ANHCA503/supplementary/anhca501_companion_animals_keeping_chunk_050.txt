E. Conformation
The conformation or shape of the horse will dictate the athletic ability and the ability to stay sound.
 Straight legs, especially through the knees and hocks, suggest that the horse will not breakdown
as soon as a horse with crooked legs.
 Body conformation and the angle at which the neck ties into the shoulder determine whether the
horse is capable of being a saddle type
 Short, strong-backed horses, horses with good angle to the shoulder, horses with long hips and
strong hind quarters are desired.
 A bright, alert head and eye, a long neck, and a deep heart girth makes horses more athletic and
consequently, attractive.

F. Size of rider and horse
The horse is capable of carrying a tremendous amount of weight. The only time the relative size of the
rider and the horse is important is when showing, then the suitability of horse to rider becomes an issue:
 Small children are better off on quite dispositional large horses than on small ponies that are wild.
 Most adults would do well to buy horses (over 147 cm at the top of the withers).
 As long as the mount is quiet enough for the child to work around and mount, the size of the
animal should be considered secondary.
 A rider's leg ought to fit down the sides of the horse's side in order to give leg cues (signals to the
horse), but not be so long that the leg from the knee down does not touch the ribs.
