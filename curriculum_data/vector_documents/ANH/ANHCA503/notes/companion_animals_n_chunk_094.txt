2. Accepting a Headstall
Once trust has been established, the next step involves introducing a headstall
to the camel. This can be stressful for an unaccustomed animal, so it’s crucial
to prepare properly by ensuring a calm environment. The handler should take
their time—this could take anywhere from 30 minutes to several hours—to
help the camel accept this new sensation positively. A calm acceptance of the
headstall sets the stage for all subsequent training steps.
3. Respecting the Rope
After successfully introducing the headstall, camels need to learn about
pressure and release while being tied securely. This teaches them that pulling
back creates discomfort while moving forward releases pressure. This self-
taught lesson is vital for developing positive leading behaviors and helps
establish respect for cues given through the headstall.
4. Learning to Sit (Hoosh)
Next, handlers will teach camels to sit on command using terms like “hoosh” or
“kush.” Sitting is a natural behavior for camels; thus, this step aligns well with
their instincts. Handlers should use gentle prompting techniques that minimize
stress while reinforcing the association between the command and action.
