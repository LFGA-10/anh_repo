D. Controlling of Undesirable Reproduction
75

Controlling undesirable reproduction in companion animals such as dogs, cats,
horses, donkeys, and camels is crucial for managing populations, preventing
unwanted litters, and addressing behavioral issues associated with breeding.
Various techniques are employed to achieve this goal, including vasectomy,
castration, and female sterilization.
Vasectomy (Male Sterilization)
Vasectomy is a surgical procedure that involves the cutting and sealing of the
vas deferens, which are the tubes that carry sperm from the testicles to the
urethra. This procedure prevents sperm from being included in the ejaculate
but does not affect hormone production or libido. As a result, males retain their
sexual behaviors but cannot reproduce.
The advantages of vasectomy include:
Retention of Sexual Behavior: Males can still exhibit normal mating behaviors
without the risk of producing offspring.
Reversibility: In some cases, vasectomies can be reversed if future breeding is
desired.
Reduced Risk of Certain Health Issues: Studies suggest that neutering can
reduce risks associated with certain cancers and infections.
However, it is important to note that vasectomy does not prevent other
reproductive health issues or diseases transmitted through mating.
Castration (Male Neutralization)
Castration refers to the surgical removal of the testicles (orchiectomy), leading
to a significant reduction in testosterone levels. This procedure effectively
eliminates a male’s ability to reproduce and alters various behaviors associated
with mating.
