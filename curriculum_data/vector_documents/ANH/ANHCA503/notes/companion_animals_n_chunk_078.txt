Cats: Cats typically rely on stealth or flight responses but can become
aggressive if cornered or threatened. Their defensive posture includes hissing
or arching their backs.
Horses: Horses utilize flight responses as prey animals; they will flee from
perceived threats but can also kick if cornered.
Donkeys: Donkeys are known for being protective of other livestock; they may
confront threats directly rather than fleeing.
Camels: Camels can exhibit aggressive behavior when threatened; they may
spit or kick at perceived dangers while also relying on flight responses in some
situations.
Intra and Interspecific Behaviors Intra-specific interactions occur between
members of the same species while interspecific interactions occur between
different species.
Dogs (Intra): Social structures among dogs often involve pack dynamics where
hierarchy plays a significant role in behavior.
(Interspecific) Dogs may interact positively with humans but can show
aggression towards unfamiliar dogs depending on socialization experiences.
Cats (Intra): Cats often establish territories within multi-cat households leading
to hierarchical structures based on age or size.
(Interspecific) Cats generally maintain distance from other species but can
coexist peacefully with humans if properly socialized.
Horses (Intra): Within horse herds, social hierarchies dictate access to
resources; dominant horses will assert themselves over others.
