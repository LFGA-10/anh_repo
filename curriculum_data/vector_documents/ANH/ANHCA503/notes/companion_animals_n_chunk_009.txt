❖ Skin and hair
It has an outer layer called the epidermis that is constantly being replaced, and
an inner layer called the dermis that contains nerves and blood vessels, oil
9

glands, and hair follicles. The oil glands secrete sebum that coats and protects
the fur, giving it a glossy sheen. Shampoos and other topical products for
people can irritate your cat's skin and should be avoided. As in dogs, cats have
small muscles attached to hair follicles, which can make hair stand erect for
temperature control or as a warning sign (that is, “raised hackles”) in response
to danger. Skin hair shedding depends on climate, nutrition, and general
overall health.
❖ Teeth and mouth
Cats are carnivores with teeth designed for piercing and tearing meat. They
have 26 deciduous (baby) teeth that are replaced by 30 permanent (adult) teeth
that erupt between 5 and 7 months of age. The front teeth, which include the
12 incisors and 4 large canine teeth (also known as eye teeth), are designed for
grasping and tearing. The rearward premolar and molar teeth grind food into
smaller pieces so that it can be swallowed.
The mouth contains the salivary glands, which lubricate food and begin
digestion. The tongue helps guide food to the back of the throat and lapping up
water. The rough tongue aids in grooming and can be used to scrape meat
away from bones.
❖ Digestive and urinary tracts
The structure and function is like other animals and digestive problems are
vomiting or diarrhea, which can have many causes, including viral infections;
worms; stress; or ingestion of bones, string, hair, or other foreign material.
The urinary system eliminates wastes from protein breakdown and helps
control fluid levels. In males, the urethra doubles as a channel for sperm
