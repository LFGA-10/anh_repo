May not meet the needs of highly active animals or those with higher energy
requirements.
Can lead to anxiety or stress in animals that are accustomed to ad libitum
feeding if they feel restricted.

c) Time Restricted Distribution
Time-restricted distribution involves offering food during specific time windows
throughout the day. This method combines elements of both ad libitum and
fixed amount distributions.
Advantages:
Provides structure while still allowing some flexibility in feeding times.
Can help regulate metabolism and prevent obesity by limiting eating periods.
Encourages routine which can be beneficial for training purposes.
Disadvantages:
May not accommodate all animals’ natural eating patterns; some may prefer
grazing throughout the day.
Requires careful planning to ensure that all nutritional needs are met within
limited time frames.
d) Forced Feeding
Forced feeding is a technique where food is administered directly into an
animal’s mouth or through a tube when they refuse to eat voluntarily. This
method is often used in clinical settings or when an animal is ill.
Advantages:
Ensures that undernourished or sick animals receive necessary nutrients when
they cannot eat on their own.
Can be life-saving in critical situations where immediate nutrition is required.
Disadvantages:
Can cause stress and trauma to the animal if not done carefully.
