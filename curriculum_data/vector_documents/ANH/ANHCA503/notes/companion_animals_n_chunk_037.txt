D. Camels
Camel Breeds
Camels are large, even-toed ungulates that belong to the family Camelidae.
They are primarily found in arid regions of the world and are known for their
ability to withstand extreme temperatures and limited water availability. There
are two main species of camels: the Dromedary (Camelus dromedarius), which
has one hump, and the Bactrian camel (Camelus bactrianus), which has two
humps.
Origin
Dromedary Camels: Originating from the Arabian Peninsula, North Africa, and
parts of South Asia, Dromedaries are well adapted to hot desert climates.
Bactrian Camels: These camels are native to Central Asia, particularly
Mongolia and China, where they thrive in cold desert environments.
Size:
Dromedary Camels: Typically stand between 1.8 to 2 meters (5.9 to 6.6 feet) at
the shoulder.
Bactrian Camels: Generally larger than Dromedaries, they can reach heights of
about 2 to 2.3 meters (6.6 to 7.5 feet) at the shoulder.
Weight
Dromedary Camels: Weigh between 400 to 600 kg (880 to 1,320 lbs).
