Clearing the Site: Remove any debris, rocks, or vegetation that may interfere
with the foundation.
Grading: The ground must be graded to create a flat surface. This can involve
cutting away high spots and filling low areas with soil to achieve an even base.
51

Compaction: Once graded, the soil must be compacted to prevent settling after
construction. This can be done using mechanical compactors or by hand tools
depending on the size of the area.
Verification: Use a level tool (such as a laser level) to ensure that the ground is
perfectly flat and meets local building codes.
Elevation
After levelling, elevation refers to how high above ground level the structure will
sit:
Foundation Type Selection: Choose an appropriate foundation type based on
soil conditions and climate (e.g., slab-on-grade, crawl space, or basement).
Each type has different elevation requirements.
Footings Installation: Footings are crucial as they support the weight of the
structure above them. They should be placed below frost lines in colder
climates to prevent heaving.
Foundation Walls: Construct walls according to specifications ensuring they
are plumb and square. Proper reinforcement with rebar may also be required
depending on local regulations.
Waterproofing: Apply waterproofing measures around foundations to protect
against moisture intrusion which could harm both structural integrity and
animal health.
Inspection: Have a qualified inspector check that all elevation aspects meet
local building codes before proceeding further.
