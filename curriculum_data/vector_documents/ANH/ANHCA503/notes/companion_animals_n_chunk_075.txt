LO3: TRAIN COMPANION ANIMALS
Companion animals training
Companion animals training refer to teaching animals specific responses to
specific conditions or stimuli. An animal trainer should consider the natural
behaviors of the animal and aim to modify behaviors through a basic system of
reward and punishment.
3.1: Description of companion animal behavior
Common Companion Animal Behaviors
Animal behavior includes all the ways animals interact with other organisms
and the physical environment.
Behavior can also be defined as a change in the activity of an organism in
response to a stimulus, an external or internal cue or combo of cues.
Feeding Behavior Feeding behaviors in companion animals such as dogs, cats,
horses, donkeys, and camels vary significantly based on their species and
domestication.
Dogs: Dogs are omnivores and exhibit scavenging behavior. They often eat
quickly and may show signs of food guarding. Their feeding can be influenced
by social dynamics within a household.
