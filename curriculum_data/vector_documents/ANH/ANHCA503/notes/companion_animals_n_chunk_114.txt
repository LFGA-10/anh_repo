In summary, diagnosing companion animal diseases is a comprehensive
process that requires careful consideration of historical data, physical
examinations both at a distance and up close, vital sign assessments, sample
collection for laboratory analysis, and interpretation of laboratory results.
4.3. Treating of companion animals
When treating companion animals, the process involves several critical steps:
selecting drugs, administering drugs, and following up with the patient. Each
of these steps requires careful consideration to ensure the health and well-
being of the animal.
A. Selecting Drugs
The selection of appropriate medications for companion animals is crucial. This
process begins with a thorough assessment of the animal’s condition, which
includes:
Diagnosis: Accurate diagnosis is essential. Veterinarians often perform physical
examinations, laboratory tests (such as blood work), imaging studies (like X-
rays or ultrasounds), and sometimes biopsies to determine the underlying
issue affecting the animal.
Understanding Pharmacology: Knowledge of pharmacology is vital in selecting
drugs. Veterinarians must consider factors such as:
The specific disease or condition being treated.
The pharmacokinetics (absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion) of
potential medications.
The pharmacodynamics (the effects and mechanisms of action) of these drugs.
