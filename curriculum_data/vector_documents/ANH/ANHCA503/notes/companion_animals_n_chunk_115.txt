Species-Specific Considerations: Different species metabolize drugs differently;
therefore, veterinarians must select medications that are safe and effective for
the specific type of companion animal being treated (e.g., dogs vs. cats).
Dosage Calculations: Determining the correct dosage based on the animal’s
weight, age, and overall health status is critical to avoid underdosing or
overdosing.
Owner Education: Educating pet owners about the chosen medication—its
purpose, how it works, potential side effects, and administration instructions—
is also an important part of drug selection.
B. Administering Drugs
Once a medication has been selected, proper administration is key to ensuring
its effectiveness:
Routes of Administration: Medications can be administered through various
routes including oral (tablets or liquids), injectable (subcutaneous,
intramuscular), topical (creams or ointments), or inhalation (nebulizers). The
route chosen depends on factors like urgency of treatment and patient
compliance.
Technique: Proper technique during administration is crucial to ensure that
the medication is delivered effectively
For oral medications, ensuring that they are swallowed can sometimes require
special techniques or treats.
Injections must be given at appropriate sites with sterile equipment to prevent
infection.
