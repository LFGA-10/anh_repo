4.1: Assessment of companion animal health status
Assessment of Companion Animal Health Status:
A. General Observation of Behavior Changes
When assessing the health status of companion animals, one of the most
critical aspects is observing changes in behavior. Behavior serves as a key
indicator of an animal’s physical and emotional well-being. Here’s a detailed
breakdown of how to assess these behavioral changes systematically:
1. Understanding Normal Behavior
Before identifying any changes, it is essential to establish what constitutes
normal behavior for the specific species and breed of the companion animal.
Normal behaviors can include:
Activity Levels: Typical energy levels during play or rest.
Social Interactions: How they interact with humans and other animals.
Eating Habits: Regularity and enthusiasm for food.
Grooming Patterns: Frequency and thoroughness of self-grooming.
2. Identifying Behavioral Changes
Once normal behavior is established, any deviations from this baseline can
indicate potential health issues. Common behavioral changes to observe
include:
Increased Aggression or Fearfulness: Sudden aggression towards people or
other pets may signal pain or discomfort.
