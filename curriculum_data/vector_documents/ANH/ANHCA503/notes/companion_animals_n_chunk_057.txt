The nutritional requirements for companion animals such as dogs, cats,
horses, donkeys, and camels vary significantly based on species, age, activity
level, and health status.

Dogs: Dogs are omnivores with a diet that should include proteins (20-30% of
their diet), fats (8-15%), carbohydrates (30-70%), vitamins, and minerals.
Essential amino acids like taurine are crucial for heart health.
Cats: Cats are obligate carnivores requiring a higher protein intake (30-50%)
and specific nutrients like taurine and arachidonic acid that they cannot
synthesize from plant sources.
Horses: Horses require a diet high in fiber (from hay or pasture) along with
grains for energy. Their nutritional needs include approximately 10-14%
protein, essential fatty acids, vitamins A, D, E, and B-complex vitamins.
Donkeys: Donkeys have lower energy requirements than horses; their diet
should consist mainly of forage with about 8-12% protein content. They also
need minerals like calcium and phosphorus in appropriate ratios.
Camels: Camels can thrive on a variety of feeds but require high-fiber diets
rich in roughage. Their protein needs range from 10-15%, depending on their
workload and physiological state.
b) Feed Ingredients
The selection of feed ingredients is critical to meet the nutritional requirements
of these animals:
Protein Sources: Common sources include meat meal (for dogs and cats),
soybean meal (for all species), alfalfa meal (for horses), and legume seeds (for
donkeys).
Carbohydrates: Grains such as corn, barley, oats for dogs and horses; rice or
potatoes can be included for dogs; while donkeys benefit from low-starch
options.
