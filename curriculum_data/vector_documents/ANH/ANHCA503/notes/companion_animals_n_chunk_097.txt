C. Using Technology

In recent years, technology has significantly advanced the ways in which pet
owners can monitor and understand their companion animals’ behavior.
Various devices and systems have been developed to provide insights into pets’
activities, health, and overall well-being. Below are some of the most prominent
technologies used for monitoring companion animal behavior:
Microchip
Microchipping is a widely adopted method for identifying pets. A microchip is a
small electronic device implanted under the skin of an animal, typically
between the shoulder blades. Each microchip contains a unique identification
number that can be read by a scanner. This technology primarily serves as a
means of identification if a pet gets lost or separated from its owner. While it
does not actively monitor behavior, it plays a crucial role in ensuring that lost
pets can be returned to their owners quickly.
Pet Cameras
Pet cameras have gained popularity among pet owners who wish to keep an eye
on their animals while away from home. These devices often come equipped
with features such as live streaming video, two-way audio communication, and
motion detection alerts. Some advanced models even include treat dispensers
that allow owners to reward their pets remotely. By using pet cameras, owners
can observe their pets’ behaviors in real-time, identify any signs of distress or
anxiety, and ensure they are safe when left alone.
