Suturing techniques
The conjunctival area at the base of the third eyelid can be carefully
sutured with 5-0 Vicryl, or not sutured and left to granulate
Complications, although rare, include
✓ orbital fat prolapses,
✓ hemorrhage,
✓ KCS or superficial keratitis,
✓ entropion, (the inversion or turning inward of the border of the
eyelid against the eyeball)
✓ conjunctivitis, and
✓ infection.
Topic 3: Eye enucleation
Enucleation is the surgical removal of the eyeball that leaves the
eye muscles and remaining orbital contents intact
Why is eye extirpation performed?
There are several reasons why a doctor might recommend an
enucleation:
Severe eye cancer: If cancer is present in the eye and can't be
•
treated with other methods, enucleation may be necessary to
prevent the spread of cancer.
Painful blind eye: In some cases, an eye may be blind and
•
cause chronic pain. Enucleation can remove the source of pain
and improve quality of life.
Severe trauma: Serious injuries to the eye that can't be
•
repaired may necessitate enucleation.
