Topic 2: Surgery of the third eyelid
Surgery of the third eyelid, also known as the nictitating membrane,
is a relatively uncommon procedure performed by veterinary
ophthalmologists. It is typically reserved for severe cases where the
function of the eyelid is impaired or when there is a mass present.
There are a few different reasons
❖ cherry eye, which is a prolapse of the gland located in the
third eyelid. This gland produces tears, and when it prolapses,
it appears as a red, cherry-like mass in the corner of the eye.
❖ tumor of the third eyelid or its gland. Third eyelid tumors can
be benign or malignant, and surgery is necessary to remove
the tumor and prevent it from spreading.
❖ third eyelid eversion, where the cartilage in the third eyelid
becomes deformed and the eyelid flips inside out. This can
cause irritation and dryness of the eye.
❖ Cyst formation
❖ Foreign bodies behind the nictitans
