Treatment (Step-by-Step Management)
a) Preparation
Restrain the animal properly (standing or in sternal recumbency).
Clean the prolapsed tissue with warm antiseptic saline to remove
dirt and fecal material.
Assess viability of tissue:
Pink, moist → viable (can be replaced)
Dark, dry, or necrotic → may require partial amputation

b) Anaesthesia
❖ Administer epidural anaesthesia to reduce straining:
❖ Cow: 5–10 ml of 2% lignocaine
❖ Sheep/Goat: 2–3 ml
❖ Pig: 2–3 ml
c) Reduction (Repositioning the Rectum)
❖ Apply osmotic agent (e.g., sugar or glycerine) for 10–15
minutes to reduce swelling and oedema.
❖ Lubricate well with sterile jelly.
❖ Gently push the prolapsed rectum back into its normal
position using the palm or fingers (never forcefully).
❖ Ensure all folds are smooth and fully reduced.
d) Retention Techniques
To prevent recurrence, use one of the following methods:
1. Purse-string Suture (most common)
Place a purse-string suture around the anus using non-absorbable
material (e.g., nylon).
Tighten enough to retain the rectum, but leave an opening for
defecation.
Keep for 5–7 days, then remove once healing occurs.
2. Horizontal Mattress or Cross Sutures
Place two or three sutures across the anus, leaving gaps for feces
to pass.
3. Retention with Band or Harness
In small animals, a rectal retention bandage or tail tie may be
used temporarily.
