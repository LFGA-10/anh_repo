5. Postoperative Care
✓ Immobilisation
✓ Apply an external splint, cast, or bandage for additional
support.
✓ Medication
✓ Administer antibiotics for 5–7 days to prevent infection.

✓ Give analgesics and anti-inflammatory drugs to relieve pain
and reduce swelling.
✓ Wound management
✓ Keep the wound clean and dry.
✓ Remove skin sutures after 10–14 days.
✓ Physiotherapy
✓ Restrict movement initially, then gradually allow mild exercise
as healing progresses.
✓ Monitoring
✓ Check for swelling, infection, or loosening of fixation devices.
✓ Take follow-up radiographs to assess bone healing.
6. Advantages of Open Reduction
✓ Direct visualization allows accurate alignment of fragments.
✓ Suitable for complex or displaced fractures.
✓ Enables internal fixation for stable and faster healing.
7. Disadvantages
❖ Requires surgical expertise and sterile conditions.
❖ Higher risk of infection and soft tissue damage.
❖ Expensive due to implants and anaesthesia.
INTERNAL FIXATION OF FRACTURES
1. Definition
Internal fixation is a surgical technique in which implants or
devices (such as pins, plates, screws, or wires) are placed inside the
body to stabilize and maintain fractured bone fragments in proper
alignment until healing occurs.
