❖ Once the rectal pouch is found, make a small incision into it

e) Haemorrhage Control
❖ Control bleeding by gentle pressure with sterile gauze.
❖ Use ligation or cauterization if necessary.
f) Suturing Techniques
❖ Suture the mucosa of the rectum to the skin margin using
simple interrupted sutures or simple continuous sutures with
absorbable material (e.g., catgut or vicryl).
❖ Ensure the opening is large enough to allow normal fecal
passage but not excessively wide.
5. Prognosis
Good, if the rectum and colon are fully formed and the surgery is
done early.
Poor, if associated with other defects (e.g., atresia of colon or
rectovaginal fistula).
Topic 4: Laparo-rumenotomy
The terms ‘coeliotomy’ and ‘laparotomy’ are both used to refer to the
surgical opening of the abdominal cavity. Coeliotomy means in
general, the opening of the abdominal cavity. Laparotomy is actually
the opening of the abdomen by means of a flank incision. A
rumenotomy is a routine surgical procedure where the rumen is
surgically incised via the left abdominal wall.
