2. Objectives
104

❖ To immobilize the fractured bone externally until healing
occurs.
❖ To maintain proper alignment of bone fragments.
❖ To protect the fracture site from further injury.
❖ To allow access to wounds in open or infected fractures.
3. Indications
External fixation is indicated when:
❖ The fracture is closed, simple, and minimally displaced (for
splints or casts).
❖ There is a compound (open) fracture that needs frequent
wound dressing.
❖ Internal fixation is not possible due to cost or infection.
❖ The fracture involves young or small animals where healing
is rapid.
❖ Temporary immobilisation is needed before surgery.
4. Types of External Fixation
A. Traditional (Conventional) Methods
❖ Splints
❖ Casts (Plaster of Paris or Fiberglass)
❖ Bandages (Robert Jones or Modified Robert Jones)
B. Modern Methods
External Skeletal Fixation (ESF) using metal pins, rods, and
clamps outside the body.
