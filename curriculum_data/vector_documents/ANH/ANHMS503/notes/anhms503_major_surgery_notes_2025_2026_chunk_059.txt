3. Rumen exteriorization: The rumen contents will be carefully
packed away, and the rumen wall itself will be gently
exteriorized (brought out of the abdominal cavity). Sutures or a

specialized rumen clamp might be used to secure the rumen

4. Access and addressing the issue: Once exteriorized, the
veterinarian will have access to the rumen. The specific
procedure will depend on the reason for the rumenotomy. Here
are some examples:
o Foreign body removal: The vet will locate and carefully
remove the foreign object.
o Bloat relief: A trocar (hollow tube) might be inserted into
the rumen to release gas buildup.
o Reticulum surgery: If needed, the veterinarian might
access the reticulum through the rumen to remove sharp
objects and address infection.
5. Rumen closure: After addressing the issue within the rumen,
the rumen contents will be carefully returned, and the rumen
wall will be sutured closed in multiple layers.
6. Abdominal wall closure: The abdominal muscle layers and
skin incision will be sutured closed in separate layers.
7. Post-surgical care: The animal will be monitored closely after
surgery for signs of infection, pain, and proper healing. Pain
medication, antibiotics, and fluids will likely be continued
post-surgery
