Mechanism (How It Happens)
46

❖ Increased intra-abdominal pressure (from straining, labor, or
coughing) pushes the organ toward the body opening.
❖ Weak or relaxed pelvic ligaments or smooth muscles fail to
hold it in place.
❖ The organ then everts (turns inside out) and protrudes
externally.
Predisposing Factors
❖ Prolonged straining during parturition or defecation
❖ Hypocalcemia causing uterine atony (loss of muscle tone)
❖ Overfeeding or high body condition in late pregnancy
❖ Hormonal imbalance (high estrogen or relaxation of pelvic
ligaments)
❖ Genetic predisposition (recurrent in some breeding lines)
❖ Infection or inflammation of reproductive or rectal tissues
Clinical Importance
❖ Prolapse is an emergency condition; the exposed tissue is
prone to drying, injury, contamination, and infection.
❖ If untreated, it can lead to shock, hemorrhage, necrosis, or
death.
❖ Early recognition and prompt treatment restore normal
function and improve survival.
