Purpose of Immobilization
❖ To maintain joint stability after reduction.
❖ To prevent re-dislocation.
❖ To reduce pain and inflammation.
❖ To promote tissue repair and recovery.

A. Traditional Splints
Definition: Splints are rigid or semi-rigid supports applied along
the limb to prevent joint movement.
Examples:
❖ Bamboo splints (common in field conditions).
❖ Wooden or metal rods, padded with cotton and bandage.
❖ PVC pipe halves cut and shaped to the limb.
Application:
❖ Place a soft padding layer (cotton or bandage) to prevent
pressure sores.
❖ Apply the splint along the limb to cover the joints above and
below the injury.
❖ Secure with bandages or adhesive tape.
Advantages:
❖ Inexpensive and easy to apply.
❖ Suitable for field use.
Disadvantages:
❖ Not as stable as casts.
❖ Requires frequent adjustment.
B. Casts
Definition: A cast is a rigid encasement made of plaster of Paris
or fiberglass that completely surrounds the limb to maintain joint
immobilization.
Application Steps:
❖ Clip and clean the limb.
❖ Apply a cotton or gauze padding evenly.
❖ Wet plaster of Paris bandages and wrap around the limb from
distal to proximal.
