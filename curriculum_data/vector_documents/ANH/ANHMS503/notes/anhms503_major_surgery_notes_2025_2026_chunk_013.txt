Cleaning Methods:

Manual cleaning: Instruments are scrubbed thoroughly with
•
a detergent or enzymatic solution to remove visible
contaminants.
Ultrasonic cleaning: This method uses high-frequency sound
•
waves to dislodge debris from crevices and complex
instruments.
Disinfection vs. Sterilization:
Disinfection: This process significantly reduces the number of
•
microorganisms on the instrument but doesn't necessarily
eliminate all spores. Disinfected instruments are suitable for
procedures that don't involve a sterile field, like changing
dressings.
Sterilization: This process destroys all microorganisms,
•
including bacteria, viruses, and spores. Sterilized instruments
are essential for surgical procedures to minimize the risk of
SSIs.
Sterilization Methods:
Autoclave: This is the most common method of sterilization in
•
healthcare settings. It uses high pressure and steam to kill
microorganisms.
Chemical sterilization: Certain chemicals can be used to
•
sterilize instruments, but this method is not as widely used
due to potential health risks and limitations on the types of
instruments it can be used for.
