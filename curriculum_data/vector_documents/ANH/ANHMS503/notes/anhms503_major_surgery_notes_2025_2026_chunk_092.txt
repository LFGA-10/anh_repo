2. Objectives
❖ To maintain accurate alignment of fractured bone ends.
❖ To provide stability for early healing.
❖ To allow early limb use and reduce complications like
malunion or non-union.
❖ To restore normal anatomy and function of the bone.
3. Indications
Internal fixation is indicated when:
❖ Closed reduction and external immobilization are not effective.
❖ There is a compound (open) or comminuted fracture.
❖ The fracture involves weight-bearing bones (e.g., femur,
humerus).
❖ The animal is large or active, making external fixation difficult.
❖ There is a need for precise anatomical alignment (e.g., joint
fractures).
4. Preoperative Preparation
Patient preparation
❖ Fasting for 12–24 hours.
❖ Clip, shave, and disinfect the surgical area.
❖ Maintain strict aseptic technique.
Anaesthesia
❖ General anaesthesia is required (e.g., xylazine + ketamine
combination).
❖ Local infiltration may assist in minor cases.
Positioning
❖ Position the animal to give easy access to the fracture site
(e.g., lateral recumbency for limb fractures).
❖ Secure the limb using sandbags, ropes, or limb supports.
