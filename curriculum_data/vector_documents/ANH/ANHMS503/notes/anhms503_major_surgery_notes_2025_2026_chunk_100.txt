Description:
A rigid frame made of pins or wires inserted through the bone
and connected externally by bars or clamps.
Components:
❖ Pins (transfixation pins): pass through the bone.
107

❖ Connecting bars: metal or carbon rods linking the pins.
❖ Clamps: secure pins to bars externally.
Procedure:
✓ Prepare and drape the surgical site aseptically.
✓ Insert fixation pins perpendicular to the bone through the
skin and bone cortex on both sides of the fracture.
✓ Attach connecting rods to the pins using clamps.
✓ Tighten all connections to create a stable external frame.
✓ Dress pin sites and monitor for infection.
Advantages:
✓ Provides excellent stability for complex fractures.
✓ Allows adjustment and wound access in open fractures.
✓ Can be removed after healing without re-opening the bone.
Disadvantages:
✓ Requires surgical skill and equipment.
✓ Risk of pin tract infection.
✓ More expensive than traditional splints.
6. Advantages of External Fixation
✓ Simple and non-invasive (no opening of fracture).
✓ Easy to apply in the field.
✓ Allows wound management in open fractures.
✓ Can be adjusted or removed easily.
7. Disadvantages
✓ Provides less rigid fixation than internal methods.
✓ Risk of loosening or soft tissue injury.
✓ Requires frequent monitoring and maintenance.
