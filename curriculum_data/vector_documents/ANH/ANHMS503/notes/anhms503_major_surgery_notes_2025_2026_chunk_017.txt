Before "knife-to-skin," the surgical team conducts a final
verification process to ensure everything is in order.
Equipment and Supplies: The team confirms that all
•
necessary equipment and instruments are present and
working correctly. This includes checking for any equipment
issues or concerns.
Counts: The surgical team, particularly the scrub nurse,
•
performs a count of all instruments, sponges, and needles to
prevent anything from being left inside the patient.
Specimen Labeling: Any specimens that will be removed
•
during surgery are pre-labeled to ensure they are correctly
identified.
Documentation: The entire process, including the surgical
•
safety checklist and any specific patient or procedure details,
is documented in the patient's medical record.
The preparation of a surgical workplace is a systematic and critical
process designed to ensure the safety of the patient and the
efficiency of the surgical team. It is a key component of
perioperative care and is often broken down into three main phases:
before the patient arrives, the immediate pre-operative phase, and
during the procedure itself.

Topic 3. Pre-Operative Preparation (Operating Room and

This phase takes place before the patient is brought into the
operating room (OR). The goal is to create a sterile and functional
environment.
