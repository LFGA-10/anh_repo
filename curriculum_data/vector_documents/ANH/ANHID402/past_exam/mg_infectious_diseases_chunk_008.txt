Trade Restrictions:

Brucellosis-free certification is often required for export; infected herds face quarantine or trade bans, affecting farmers economically.

Veterinary and Control Costs:

Expenses for testing, vaccination, culling, and biosecurity measures increase production costs.

2. Zoonotic Importance

Brucellosis is a major zoonotic disease, meaning it can spread from animals to humans:

Transmission to Humans:

Through consumption of unpasteurized milk, cheese, or other dairy products.

Direct contact with infected animal tissues, aborted fetuses, or fluids.

Occupational exposure in farmers, veterinarians, abattoir workers, and laboratory personnel.

Human Health Impact:

Causes undulating fever, malaise, joint pain, and chronic illness.

Can lead to long-term complications like arthritis, endocarditis, or infertility.

Public Health Costs:

Treatment in humans requires prolonged antibiotics, increasing healthcare costs.

Lost productivity due to illness affects both families and communities.

A farmer found two cows dead with blood oozing from the nose and mouth. No rigor mortis was observed.
Questions:

What is the most likely disease?

Why should the carcass not be opened?

What sample is safe to collect for laboratory confirmation?

List two differential diagnosis of the expected diseases.

What emergency control measures should be implemented?

ANSWER

1. Most Likely Disease

Anthrax (Bacillus anthracis)

Sudden death, hemorrhage from natural openings, and lack of rigor mortis are characteristic signs.

2. Why the Carcass Should Not Be Opened

Opening the carcass can release large numbers of spores into the environment.
