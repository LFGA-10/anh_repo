2. Why the Carcass Should Not Be Opened

Opening the carcass can release large numbers of spores into the environment.

Anthrax spores are highly resistant and infectious, posing serious risk to humans and other animals.

Safe handling requires strict biosafety procedures.

3. Safe Sample for Laboratory Confirmation

Blood from natural openings (nose, mouth, or ear) collected using sterile containers.

Avoid tissue samples unless strict biosafety measures are in place.

Smears can be examined with Gram stain or M’Fadyean stain for encapsulated bacilli.

4. Differential Diagnoses

Blackleg (Clostridium chauvoei) – sudden death, often with muscle emphysema.

Hemorrhagic septicemia (Pasteurella multocida) – sudden death with nasal discharge and swelling.

5. Emergency Control Measures

Immediate Quarantine:

Restrict movement of animals, people, and equipment in the area.

Report to Veterinary Authorities:

Anthrax is a notifiable disease.

Safe Carcass Disposal:

Burn the carcass completely or bury deep with lime.

Do not open the carcass.

Vaccination:

Vaccinate at-risk livestock with Sterne strain vaccine where available.

Disinfection:

Treat contaminated sites with 10% formalin or bleach.

Personal Protection:

Use gloves, masks, and protective clothing when handling suspected animals or samples.
