Clean the Injection Site:

Shave and disinfect the area.

Measure Initial Skin Thickness:

Use calipers to record baseline thickness.

Inject Tuberculin:

Inject 0.1 mL of bovine PPD intradermally into one site (test site).

Inject 0.1 mL of avian PPD intradermally at a separate site (control site) on the same side of the neck.

Wait 72 Hours:

Do not disturb the site during this period.

Measure Skin Thickness Again:

Use calipers to measure swelling at both sites.

4. Interpretation

Positive Reaction:

Increase in skin thickness at the bovine PPD site is ≥4 mm more than the avian PPD site (criteria may vary by country).

Indicates cell-mediated immunity to M. bovis.

Negative Reaction:

No significant swelling (increase <2 mm) at the bovine site compared to the avian site.

Inconclusive / Suspicious:

Intermediate reactions (increase 2–4 mm) – repeat testing or further diagnostics recommended.

Discuss the economic and zoonotic importance of Brucellosis in livestock.

ANSWER

1. Economic Importance

Brucellosis, caused by Brucella species (commonly B. abortus in cattle, B. melitensis in sheep and goats), leads to significant losses in livestock production:

Reproductive Losses:

Causes abortion, stillbirth, weak offspring, and infertility.

Leads to reduced herd replacement and decreased calving rates.

Decreased Milk Production:

Infected animals often have mastitis, reducing milk yield and quality.

Reduced Growth and Weight Gain:

Chronic infection can cause poor body condition and slow growth.

Trade Restrictions:

Brucellosis-free certification is often required for export; infected herds face quarantine or trade bans, affecting farmers economically.
