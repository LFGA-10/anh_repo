Questions:

What disease is suspected?

Explain the major transmission route in this disease.

How can this disease affect humans?

How should infected animals be handled?

What laboratory tests can confirm the infection?

ANSWERS

1. Suspected Disease

Brucellosis (Brucella abortus in cattle)

Characterized by late-term abortions, retained placenta, and infertility.

2. Major Transmission Route

Animal-to-animal transmission:

Contact with aborted fetuses, placenta, vaginal discharges, or milk from infected cows.

Environmental contamination:

Brucella bacteria can survive in soil, water, and feed for weeks.

Venereal transmission through infected bulls is also possible.

3. Human Health Impact

Brucellosis is zoonotic. Humans can be infected by:

Handling infected animals, fetuses, or placentas without protection.

Consuming unpasteurized milk or dairy products.

Causes undulating fever, malaise, joint pain, and chronic illness in humans.

4. Handling of Infected Animals

Isolate affected animals to prevent spread.

Wear protective clothing, gloves, and masks when handling infected material.

Do not consume milk or meat from infected animals unless properly treated.

Vaccinate susceptible animals in endemic areas.

Culling may be required in severe or persistent outbreaks under veterinary guidance.

5. Laboratory Tests for Confirmation

Serological Tests:

Rose Bengal Test (RBT) – rapid screening

Complement Fixation Test (CFT) – confirmation

ELISA – sensitive detection of antibodies

Bacteriological Culture:

Isolation of Brucella from aborted fetus, placenta, vaginal discharge, or milk
