Briefly describe how are different reagents used during a Gram staining laboratory test?

Answer (with marks allocations)

Crystal Violet (Primary Stain: applied for 1 minutes, it is used for bacterial smear, then rinsed with water.

Iodine (Mordant): applied for 1 minute after Crystal violet then rinsed with water.

Ethanol or Acetone (Decolorizer): Applied for 10–30 seconds, then rinsed with water.

Safranin (Counterstain): applied for 1 minute after ethanol then rinsed with water.

Explain the principle behind the Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) technique used for virus detection in the laboratory. How does it help in identifying viral infections?

Answer (with marks allocations)

The ELISA technique is based on the principle of antigen-antibody interaction.

It uses specific antibodies to detect viral antigens or antibodies produced in response to a viral infection. In a typical ELISA:

A viral antigen is immobilized on a surface.

A patient's sample (e.g., blood) is added, and if antibodies against the virus are present, they bind to the antigen.

An enzyme-linked secondary antibody is added, which binds to the primary antibody.

A substrate is added, and the enzyme converts it into a detectable signal (e.g., color change).
