52. (Evaluating) Restraint options for aggressive bull (10 marks)
Distribution of marks:

• Physical restraint evaluation: 3 marks
• Chemical restraint evaluation: 3 marks
• Comparison and recommendation: 2 marks
• Justification: 2 marks
Expected answer structure:
Physical Restraint Options:
• Squeeze chute - Advantages: Complete immobilization, safe for handler; Disadvantages:
Requires bull entry, stress
• Head gate + nose lead - Advantages: Head control; Disadvantages: Limited body access,
risk if bull goes down
• Rope casting (Burley method) - Advantages: Can recumbency; Disadvantages: Requires
skilled team, risk of injury
Chemical Restraint Options:
• Xylazine - Advantages: Sedation and analgesia; Disadvantages:
Cardiovascular/respiratory effects, withdrawal period
• Acepromazine - Advantages: Mild sedation; Disadvantages: NOT recommended in bulls
(penis prolapsus risk)
• Combination protocols - Advantages: Balanced effect; Disadvantages: Requires
veterinary expertise
Evaluation:
• Safety: Physical (squeeze chute) safest for handler; chemical safer for animal
• Welfare: Chemical reduces stress if done properly; physical may cause fear
• Effectiveness: Both effective if used correctly
• Practicality: Physical quicker; chemical requires waiting time
Recommendation:
For an aggressive bull requiring treatment:
1. Primary approach: Well-maintained squeeze chute with head gate
2. If procedure painful/prolonged: Add chemical restraint (NOT acepromazine)
