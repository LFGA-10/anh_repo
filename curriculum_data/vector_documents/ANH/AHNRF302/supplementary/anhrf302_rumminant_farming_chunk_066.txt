absorbing enough calcium from the diet or has not started mobilizing bone calcium to meet the
increased calcium demand of lactation. Calcium losses from lactation coupled with inadequate supply
results in a drop in blood calcium level. Since calcium is needed for muscle contraction, cows suffering
from milk fever often lose their ability to stand.
 Clinical Signs
There are three progressive stages of parturient paresis.
During stage one; cows are able to stand but show signs of hypersensitivity and excitability. Cows may
appear restless and bellowing. If calcium therapy is not instituted, cows will progress to stage two.

In stage two, cows are unable to stand but can maintain sternal recumbency. Depression, anorexia, dry
muzzle, subnormal body temperature, and cold extremities are seen. Cows often tuck their heads into
their flanks or, if the head is extended, an S-shaped curve to the neck may be noted.
In stage three, cows lose consciousness progressively to the point of coma. They are unable to maintain
sternal recumbency, unresponsive to stimuli, and can suffer severe bloat. Cardiac output worsens, heart
rate can approach 120 beats /min, and pulse may be undetectable. Cows in stage three may survive only
a few hours.
