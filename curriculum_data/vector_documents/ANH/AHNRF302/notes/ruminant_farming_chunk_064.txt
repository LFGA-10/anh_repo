B.3. Proper shelter and bedding
The young ones must be transferred to the calf pens that were
prepared for them. Dry straw needs to be replaced after a few days
and the place cleaned. The calf pen should have adequate space,
well ventilated and well lit.
B.4. Colostrum feeding
For the first 3-4 days after an animal give birth it produces special
milk called colostrum. It gives energy to baby animals. And
colostrum has antibodies in that help the baby animal to fight off
infection. It is better to help the calf to stand and to suckle
colostrum.
A new-born animal can only use antibodies from colostrum within
six hours after it is born. After the animal is about three weeks old
it starts to make own antibodies. It is very important for a new-
born animal to get enough colostrum to drink from mother in the
first few hours of its life.
In addition, make sure the baby animal sucks its mother’s teat as
soon as possible. When the mother feels a baby sucking, her brain
releases a hormone called oxytocin into her blood that makes milk
flow from udder. It also makes the uterus contract and pushes the
placenta out.
