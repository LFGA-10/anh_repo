B.3. Finishing

This phase of beef production consists of full feeding of grain with limited amounts of roughage until market weight and finish are reached. Older cattle may reach finish on pasture alone (or with only a few pounds of grain/day) or after 60–90 days in the feedlot on high-grain rations to improve market grade and to remove any yellow tinges from their body fat (due to stored carotene from pasture forage). Weanling calves commonly are shipped directly to the feedlot and fed finishing rations for 100–150 days; yearlings require ~150 days, and older steers 100–125 days. Grain consumption of cattle on fullfeed is1 kg/45 kg body wt. Roughage consumption usually is limited to about one-fourth to one-third of the total concentrate consumption after cattle are on full-feed. Cattle consume ~3% of their body weight/day when self-fed mixed rations. For calves, <1 kg) of a 33% protein supplement is required daily for best gains and market grades when no legume roughage is fed.

The grain (concentrate) allowance for finishing cattle should be increased gradually over 2–3 week from the time they are started on a finishing program to get them on full feed. Feeding too much grain to finishing cattle too rapidly can lead to lactic acidosis or founder .Self-fed, total mixed rations should contain >50% roughage as cattle are started on feed.
