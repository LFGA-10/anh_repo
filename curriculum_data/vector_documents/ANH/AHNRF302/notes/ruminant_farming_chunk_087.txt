A.5. Feeding dry cow
The nutrients need of dry cows are generally not as high as during
lactation. Over feeding will result in cows getting too fat during the

dry period. Limit the amount of corn silage fed because of its high
energy content. The following conditions must be respected.
✓ Maintain a total dry matter intake during this period at about
2% of body weight
✓ Roughage intake at about 1% of body weight
✓ The amount of grain depends on the quality and type of forage
fed
✓ Supplementation with Vitamin A, D, minerals such Ca and P
reduce problems with retained placenta and milk fever.
A.6. Feeding bulls
Yearling bulls should be in condition score 6 (1 = thin and
emaciated; 9 = obese) before the start of the breeding season. This
is usually not a problem as these bulls would be coming off of a
growing/development ration. Yearlings should after removal from
the cow herd after the breeding season; yearlings should be kept
separate from the older bulls, if possible, at least through their
second winter. Yearlings need to be fed or grazed on a good quality
forage or pasture. Be left with the cow herd for 60 days or less.
Beyond that time their condition can fall off to such a degree that
it could have long-range effects upon their growth.
For spring/summer breeding programs, this could be meadow
regrowth or brome grass pasture. Their supplemental feeding
regime can be equated to the program for bred-yearling heifers.
