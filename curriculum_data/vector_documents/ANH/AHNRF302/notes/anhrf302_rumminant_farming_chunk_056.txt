As soon as a baby animal is born, make sure that it is breathing.

If the baby is having difficulty breathing, hang it upside down. Rub the chest to make any fluid in the lungs come out of the nose;

Lift the new-born animal up by the back legs for a minute or so to let mucus and fluid drain out from the lungs. You can hold the back legs of a small animal and swing it round to make mucus come out of the nose;

You can also c lean mucous away from the mouth and nostril by hand.

Pull the new-born animal to where the mother can reach it easily to lick it dry and put the newborn so that it sits up.

Put a piece of dry grass up the baby animal’s nose. This makes it cough then it starts breathing.  Take the weight to determine the birth weight

B.2. Umbilical cord cutting and disinfection

The umbilical cord usually breaks on its own and is not a problem. If a long cord is still attached to the new-born animal tie a sterile string round it and cut it to about 3 cm long.

Keeps the navel of a new-born animal clean to stop infection getting in. It is a good idea to put antiseptic or antibiotic powder on the navel to prevent infection. A good way is to soak the navel in antiseptic (iodine or Dettol).

B.3. Proper shelter and bedding

The young ones must be transferred to the calf pens that were prepared for them. Dry straw needs to be replaced after a few days and the place cleaned. The calf pen should have adequate space, well ventilated and well lit.

B.4. Colostrum feeding

For the first 3-4 days after an animal give birth it produces special milk called colostrum. It gives energy to baby animals. And colostrum has antibodies in that help the baby animal to fight off infection. It is better to help the calf to stand and to suckle colostrum.
