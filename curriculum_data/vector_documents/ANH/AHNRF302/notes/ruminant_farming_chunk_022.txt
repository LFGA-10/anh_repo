B. Non-traditional system
B.1. Ranching or Commercial system
This system of production involves raising animals entirely in
buildings from which they cannot leave or in well controlled
ranges. They are fully and properly fed by the farmer, from
industrial feed or fodder crops. This type of farming requires
considerable investment (feeding, sanitary monitoring, use of
exotic breeds, housing) and is therefore very expensive.
However, its productivity must be very high to ensure the
profitability. Veterinary monitoring is intensive as well, since
animals are continuously monitored by the farmer; and in
addition, epidemic spread risks are very high because animals are
continuously put together.
For those two reasons, this type of farming is mainly practiced for
high yielding exotic breeds. Ranching systems consist of labour-
extensive enterprises specializing in one or more livestock species
and producing mainly live animals for slaughter (for meat, skins
and hides), but also for wool and milk. Management is
characterized by grazing within the fixed boundaries that delimit
tenure. Ranches are generally commercial enterprises, with
generation of a cash income as the primary function of the
livestock raised on them.
