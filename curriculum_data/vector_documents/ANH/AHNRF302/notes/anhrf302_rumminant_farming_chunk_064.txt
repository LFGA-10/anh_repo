In stage two, cows are unable to stand but can maintain sternal recumbency. Depression, anorexia, dry muzzle, subnormal body temperature, and cold extremities are seen. Cows often tuck their heads into their flanks or, if the head is extended, an S-shaped curve to the neck may be noted.

In stage three, cows lose consciousness progressively to the point of coma. They are unable to maintain sternal recumbency, unresponsive to stimuli, and can suffer severe bloat. Cardiac output worsens, heart rate can approach 120 beats /min, and pulse may be undetectable. Cows in stage three may survive only a few hours.

Treatment

The most common treatment is slowly applying an intravenous injection of a calcium gluconate solution. Single dose bottles are typical and are available at local veterinary clinics and supply stores. Calcium may also be provided orally as calcium propionate in a gel form. Retreatment is necessary in some cases.

Prevention

Numerous steps can be taken to prevent milk fever. The first is to evaluate the calcium and phosphorus levels of the diet. Excessive dietary calcium during late pregnancy could leave the cow unprepared to absorb or mobilize (resorb from bone) enough calcium to meet elevated requirements when lactation starts.
