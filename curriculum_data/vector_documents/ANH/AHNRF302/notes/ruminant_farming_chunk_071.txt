Infection gets into the uterus through the vulva and vagina,
especially when an animal gives birth in dirty place. Animals often
get metritis when the placenta does not come out.

The treatment of metritis is done immediately when the animal
shows the above sign(white /yellow discharge or a bad smell) and
fever. Give a general anti-biotherapy by injection or put antibiotic
tables into the uterus by hand but this is not the best way to treat
metritis. Even severe infection can be treated.
Prevention of metritis
✓ Make sure the place where an animal gives birth in clean and
dry. Do not always use the same place without cleaning it up.
✓ Wash your hands and arms carefully with soap or disinfectant
before putting them into an animal to help with a birth. You
can cause infection if your hands and arms are not clean.
Animals often get metritis after a difficult birth because the vagina
or cervix has been damaged.
 Pyometra
✓ Etiology Trueperella pyogenes, formerly known as
Arcanobacterium pyogenes, Actinomyces pyogenes, or
Corynebacterium pyogenes
✓ Clinical findings. Secretion from infected quarters is
initially watery with clots and later purulent. Initially severe
systemic signs including fever, inappetence, tachycardia,
depression, and mortality rate up to 50%.
✓ Treatment Responds poorly to treatment with parenteral
procaine penicillin G or oxytetracycline and intra mammary
penicillin. Affected quarter is almost always lost for milk
production.
