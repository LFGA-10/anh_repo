In dairy management, particular cows are selected and used to suckle several calves they did not produce. In order to succeed, they should be fed while suckling to limit chances of rejecting foreign calves. This practice saves labor and reduces incidence of digestive disorders experienced with bucket feeding. The dam from which all the calves suckle is referred to as foster mother. Limitations include easy spread of diseases such as Tuberculosis if the mother is infected. It is difficult to serve the calves according to their needs.

Introduction of solid feed and forage feeding

In ruminants the young ones have an under developed rumen, therefore have to depend on only milk for the first two weeks. A ration made up of forage fed free choice and limited grain may be fed. As from 7 days of age early weaner pellets are introduced alongside the milk. Water is also provided all the time ad libitum. At about 2 weeks of age, young, quality pastures are selectively cut and provided to the calves. At ten weeks calves are taken to paddocks to start direct grazing.
