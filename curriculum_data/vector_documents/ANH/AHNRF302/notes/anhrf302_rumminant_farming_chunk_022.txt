B. Non-traditional system

B.1. Ranching or Commercial system

This system of production involves raising animals entirely in buildings from which they cannot leave or in well controlled ranges. They are fully and properly fed by the farmer, from industrial feed or fodder crops. This type of farming requires considerable investment (feeding, sanitary monitoring, use of exotic breeds, housing) and is therefore very expensive.

However, its productivity must be very high to ensure the profitability. Veterinary monitoring is intensive as well, since animals are continuously monitored by the farmer; and in addition, epidemic spread risks are very high because animals are continuously put together.

For those two reasons, this type of farming is mainly practiced for high yielding exotic breeds. Ranching systems consist of labour-extensive enterprises specializing in one or more livestock species and producing mainly live animals for slaughter (for meat, skins and hides), but also for wool and milk. Management is characterized by grazing within the fixed boundaries that delimit tenure. Ranches are generally commercial enterprises, with generation of a cash income as the primary function of the livestock raised on them.

B.2. Rotational system

Paddocking (Rotational grazing) involves fencing a pasture into several small paddocks. Subdivision is a useful way to balance livestock needs with forage supply .The recommended paddock size is 1ha but this can be varied depending on the land available and the number of animals on the farm.
