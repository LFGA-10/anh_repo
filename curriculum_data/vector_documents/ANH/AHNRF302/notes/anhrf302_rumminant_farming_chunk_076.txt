A.6. Feeding bulls

Yearling bulls should be in condition score 6 (1 = thin and emaciated; 9 = obese) before the start of the breeding season. This is usually not a problem as these bulls would be coming off of a growing/development ration. Yearlings should after removal from the cow herd after the breeding season; yearlings should be kept separate from the older bulls if possible at least through their second winter. Yearlings need to be fed or grazed on a good quality forage or pasture. Be left with the cow herd for 60 days or less. Beyond that time their condition can fall off to such a degree that it could have longrange effects upon their growth.

For spring/summer breeding programs, this could be meadow regrowth or brome grass pasture. Their supplemental feeding regime can be equated to the program for bred-yearling heifers. These bulls are growing rapidly, in addition to replacing the condition they lost during the breeding pasture. Extra care and feed of yearling bulls after the breeding season should increase their longevity. These bulls should also have a Breeding Soundness Evaluation well before the start of the breeding season.
