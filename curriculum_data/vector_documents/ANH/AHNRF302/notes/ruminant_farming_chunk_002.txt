LO 1.1 – Identify cattle breeds
Introduction
Ruminants are characterized by their "four" stomachs and "cud-
chewing" behavior. The cud is a food bolus that has been
regurgitated. There are about 150 different ruminant species
including cows, goats, deer, buffalo, bison, giraffe, moose, and elk.
Ruminant species can further be classified as grazers, browsers, or
intermediates. Grazers, such as sheep, cattle, and buffalo consume
mostly lower quality grasses while browsers such as moose and
mule deer stay in the woods and eat highly nutritious twigs and
shrubs. Intermediates, such as goats and white-tailed deer have
nutritional requirements midway between grazers and browsers.
The primary difference between ruminants and simple-stomached
animals, like people, dogs or pigs, is the presence of a four-
compartment stomach that includes the rumen, reticulum,
omasum, and abomasum. Often, it is said that ruminants have
"four" stomachs. Horses are also not ruminants; however, they have
