1. What are ruminants and why is breed identification important?

Answer: Ruminants are mammals that have a specialized stomach with four compartments, enabling them to digest plant material through fermentation before it is fully digested. Examples include cattle, sheep, goats, and deer. Identifying ruminant breeds is important for breeding, health management, milk and meat production, and adapting animals to different environmental conditions.

2. What are the major cattle breeds commonly identified worldwide?

Answer: Some of the major cattle breeds include:

Holstein: Known for high milk production and white with black spots.

Angus: Renowned for high-quality beef and solid black or red color.

Hereford: Famous for beef production with a white face and red body.

Charolais: A white cattle breed known for its beef production.

Jersey: A small breed known for rich milk with high butterfat content.

3. How can one distinguish between sheep breeds?

Answer: Sheep breeds can often be distinguished by their size, wool type, and color patterns. For instance:

Merino: Known for fine wool and a wrinkled body.

Suffolk: Has black faces and legs with white bodies.

Dorper: A South African breed known for its hardiness and white body with a black head.

Romney: A wool breed with a broad body and white fleece.

4. What characteristics are used to identify goat breeds?

Answer: Goat breeds are often identified by their coat color, size, and use (meat, milk, or fiber). For example:

Nubian: A large, dairy breed with long ears and a variety of coat colors.

Boer: A meat breed with a white body and red head.

Angora: Known for producing mohair with a distinct curly fleece.

Alpine: A hardy dairy breed with erect ears and various color patterns.
