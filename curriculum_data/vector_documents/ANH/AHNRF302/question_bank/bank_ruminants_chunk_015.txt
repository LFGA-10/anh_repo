2. How does a Pastoral Ruminant Production System differ from a Traditional System?

Answer: The Pastoral System is a form of traditional livestock farming but with more mobility. Pastoralists raise herds of livestock such as cattle, sheep, and goats, and migrate with them to find pasture and water sources. Unlike the Traditional System, which may be more sedentary and dependent on fixed resources, pastoral systems often involve seasonal or nomadic movement across large areas, responding to climate and forage availability.

3. What are the advantages of an Agro-Pastoral Ruminant Production System?

Answer: The Agro-Pastoral system integrates both crop and livestock production, where animals are raised alongside crops. The system offers several advantages:

Diversification of income sources from both crops and livestock.

Manure from animals can be used to fertilize crops, improving soil fertility.

Crop residues provide additional feed for livestock, reducing the need for external feed sources.

Increased resilience to market fluctuations or climate variability due to the combination of farming and livestock.

4. What distinguishes a Non-Traditional Ruminant Production System from traditional systems?

Answer: Non-Traditional Ruminant Production Systems typically involve higher levels of technological inputs and management practices compared to traditional systems. These systems focus on increasing productivity through advanced practices such as:

Intensive feeding systems.

Use of improved breeds.

Mechanized farming equipment.
