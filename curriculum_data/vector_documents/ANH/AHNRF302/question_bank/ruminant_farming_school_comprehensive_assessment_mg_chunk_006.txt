**Risks:**

*   **Loss of Hardy Traits:** As the proportion of Friesian genetics increases, the animals may lose the Ankole's resilience to local diseases, parasites, and harsh environmental conditions. This could lead to higher veterinary costs and mortality.

*   **Increased Management Needs:** Crossbreds, especially with higher Friesian content, will likely require better quality feed, more intensive housing, and overall superior management to express their genetic potential for milk production. The farmer's current system may be inadequate.

*   **Higher Input Costs:** The program will lead to increased costs for feed, healthcare, and possibly infrastructure, which must be offset by the income from extra milk to be profitable.

**Conclusion:** The strategy can be highly beneficial if the farmer is prepared for the increased investment in management and inputs. However, if the farm operates with minimal inputs, a full upgrading program might lead to animals that are poorly adapted to the system. A cautious approach, perhaps stopping at the F1 or F2 generation, might offer a better balance between productivity and hardiness.
