Genotypic Characteristics: Certain genetic traits, such as disease resistance, feed conversion efficiency, and reproductive traits, directly influence productivity. For example, some sheep breeds may have genetic traits for higher milk yield or greater resistance to specific diseases, making them more productive in certain regions.

By selecting sheep with the appropriate phenotypic and genotypic characteristics for specific environmental conditions, farmers can maximize the efficiency of wool, meat, and milk production.

6. What are the main socio-economic roles of sheep, and how do they impact rural communities?

Answer:
Sheep provide multiple socio-economic benefits to rural communities, including:

Wool Production: Sheep like the Merino are raised for fine wool, which is a high-demand commodity in the global textile industry.

Meat and Milk: Sheep are also valued for their meat (lamb and mutton) and milk, which is used in cheese production (e.g., feta, manchego).

Manure and Soil Fertility: Sheep manure is an essential natural fertilizer for crops and pastures.

Cultural and Religious Importance: In many cultures, sheep are used in religious sacrifices, and their wool is integral to traditional weaving practices.

Sheep farming is often more sustainable and manageable in mountainous and arid regions, providing essential livelihoods for farmers and contributing to the local economy through agricultural products.
