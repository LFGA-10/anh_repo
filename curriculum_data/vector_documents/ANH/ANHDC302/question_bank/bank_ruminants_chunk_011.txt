4. What are the primary phenotypic and genotypic characteristics used to identify different sheep breeds?

Answer:

Phenotypic Characteristics:

Wool Type and Quality: Some sheep breeds are known for fine wool (e.g., Merino), while others are primarily raised for meat.

Body Size and Shape: Meat breeds tend to have more robust body structures, while wool breeds may have smaller frames.

Color and Markings: Sheep can vary in coat color from white to black or brown, and some breeds have distinctive markings (e.g., the Jacob sheep has two sets of horns).

Horns: Some breeds have horns (e.g., Dorset), while others are naturally polled (without horns).

Genotypic Characteristics:

Genotypic characteristics are related to the genetic makeup of sheep, including traits for wool production, disease resistance, and fertility. These traits are often studied through genetic testing to improve breeding programs.

The identification of sheep breeds involves both visual inspection (phenotypic) and genetic testing (genotypic), especially for breed improvement programs.

5. How do the phenotypic and genotypic characteristics influence the productivity of sheep in different environments?

Answer:

Phenotypic Characteristics: Sheep breeds with thicker wool or denser fleece are more suited to colder climates, like the Merino, which is highly prized for wool. In contrast, sheep like the Barbary are adapted to hotter environments due to their lighter wool and more heat-resistant traits.
