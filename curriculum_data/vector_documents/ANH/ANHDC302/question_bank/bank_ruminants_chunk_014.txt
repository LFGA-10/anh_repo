The large body size and high milk yield of Bos taurus cattle are key to dairy farming, while Bos indicus cattle’s heat tolerance and disease resistance make them valuable in tropical farming systems.

Goats: Goats are more adaptable than cattle and sheep and are often raised in less intensive systems. Their phenotypic characteristics, like smaller body size and resilience to harsh conditions, make them valuable for smallholder farming. Genotypically, breeds like the Saanen are selected for high milk yield, while others are chosen for fiber or meat production.

Sheep: Sheep are raised primarily for wool, meat, and milk. The genetic makeup of sheep determines wool quality and disease resistance, while their size and body shape affect meat production. Breeds like Merino are prized for wool, while meat breeds like Suffolk are selected for their large frame and high-quality meat.

The role of each species is influenced by their phenotypic traits (such as size, coat type, and milk yield) and genotypic traits (like disease resistance, fertility, and feed efficiency), which vary depending on the environmental conditions and agricultural practices of the region.

1. What are the key characteristics of a Traditional Ruminant Production System?

Answer: A Traditional Ruminant Production System typically involves low-input and low-output farming practices. It is mostly practiced in rural areas where farmers rely on indigenous knowledge and limited technological inputs. Livestock are often kept in family-based, small-scale herds and graze on communal land. This system is heavily dependent on natural pastures and crop residues. It is common in subsistence farming, where the main goal is to provide food security rather than maximize production.
