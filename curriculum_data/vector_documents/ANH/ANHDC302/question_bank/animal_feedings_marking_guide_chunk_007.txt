plain three factors that affect the freshness and viability of stored eggs before incubation.

ANSWER

1. Temperature

Description: Eggs must be stored at a cool, stable temperature, typically 55–65°F

Explanation: If eggs are stored too cold, the embryo may be damaged or growth slowed; if too warm, the embryo may start developing prematurely or bacterial growth may increase, reducing viability.

2. Humidity

Description: Ideal relative humidity for stored eggs is around 70–75%.

Explanation: Proper humidity prevents excessive moisture loss from the egg. Too low humidity causes dehydration of the egg and the embryo, while too high humidity can encourage mold and bacterial contamination.

3. Storage Duration (Age of Eggs)

Description: Fertile eggs should ideally be incubated within 7 days of laying, though some can be stored up to 10–14 days with care.

Explanation: The longer eggs are stored, the lower the hatchability. As eggs age, the albumen thins, the yolk membrane weakens, and the embryo becomes less viable.

What are the key considerations when storing feed ingredients to prevent spoilage and nutrient loss and explain each?

ANSWERS

1. Moisture Content

Consideration: Keep feed ingredients dry, ideally below 12–15% moisture for grains and below 18% for long-term storage.

Explanation: High moisture promotes mold growth, bacterial contamination, and nutrient degradation. Proper drying reduces the risk of spoilage.

2. Temperature Control

Consideration: Store feeds in a cool, well-ventilated area, away from direct sunlight or heat sources.

Explanation: High temperatures accelerate chemical reactions and rancidity (especially in fats) and can reduce vitamin content.
