Q6: Design a checklist for evaluating potential sites for a ruminant shelter.
Answer:

Drainage quality (well-drained or not).

Proximity to water sources.

Accessibility to markets and veterinary services.

Protection from predators and theft.

Availability of shade and windbreaks.

2. Selection of Construction Materials for Ruminant Shelters

Remembering

Q7: Which of the following is a common wall material for ruminant shelters?
a) Plastic sheets
b) Concrete blocks
c) Glass panels
d) Cardboard
Answer: b) Concrete blocks

Understanding

Q8: Why is concrete often used for flooring in ruminant shelters?
a) It is cheap and easy to install
b) It is durable and easy to clean
c) It provides insulation
d) It is aesthetically pleasing
Answer: b) It is durable and easy to clean

Applying

Q9: A farmer wants to build a low-cost shelter for goats. Which roofing material would be most suitable?
a) Asbestos sheets
b) Thatch
c) Metal sheets
d) Glass
Answer: b) Thatch

Analyzing

Q10: Compare the advantages and disadvantages of using metal sheets versus thatch for roofing in ruminant shelters.
Answer:

Metal sheets: Durable, fire-resistant, but expensive and can get hot.

Thatch: Low-cost, provides insulation, but less durable and prone to fire hazards.

Evaluating

Q11: Evaluate the use of wooden flooring in a ruminant shelter.
Answer: Wooden flooring is not ideal because it absorbs moisture, is difficult to clean, and can harbor pathogens, leading to health issues for the animals.
