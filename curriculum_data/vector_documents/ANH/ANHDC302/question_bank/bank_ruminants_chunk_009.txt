1. What are the main characteristics of cattle breeds, and how do they differ in their socio-economic roles?

Answer:
Cattle breeds are generally categorized into two major species: Bos taurus (taurins) and Bos indicus (zebus).

Bos taurus (Taurins): These cattle have a larger body frame, are typically adapted to cooler climates, and are more efficient for beef and dairy production in temperate regions. Their main characteristics include larger size, high milk production, and faster growth rate.

Bos indicus (Zebus): Known for their hump over the shoulders, these cattle are adapted to hot, arid climates. They are more resistant to heat, parasites, and diseases compared to taurins. They are often used in tropical and subtropical regions for milk, meat, and draught purposes.

Socio-economically, cattle play crucial roles in agriculture by providing milk, meat, hides, and labor. In some cultures, they are also seen as a status symbol or used in traditional practices.

2. What are the key differences between Bos taurus and Bos indicus regarding their characteristics?

Answer:

Body Structure: Bos taurus generally has a larger body size with a more compact frame, while Bos indicus has a smaller frame with a characteristic hump over the shoulders.

Adaptation: Bos taurus is suited to cooler climates, whereas Bos indicus is highly adapted to hot and humid tropical climates. Zebus are more heat-tolerant and have sweat glands that help with thermoregulation.
