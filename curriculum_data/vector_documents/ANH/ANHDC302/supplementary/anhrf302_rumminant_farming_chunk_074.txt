 Digestion in the young calf
A new born ruminant’s digestive tract functions primarily as monogastric digestive system. The rumen
and reticulum at the birth are small and are essentially non-functional. The abomasum/true stomach is
the largest of the four stomach compartments of the first few weeks of life. The development of rumen

and reticulum is affected by the types of diet. The calf kept on a liquid diet of milk or milk replacers may
take over 3 mouths to fully develop their reticulum function. The reticular groove also called the
esophageal groove is a trough in the wall of the reticulum that extends from the esophageal opening to
the omasum. When the calf nurses or suckles, the muscles associated with the groove contract and form
a tubular structure that conveys swallowed from the esophageal opening directly to the omasum,
essentially by passing the rumen and reticulum. The suckling act seems to be necessary for this groove
to function. With age and development of the rumen and reticulum, this groove formation reflex almost
completely disappears.
 Bucket milk feeding
The procedure of this technique is:
 Starve the calf for two to four hours to step up appetite
 Obtain warm milk from the mother and dilute 25% of its volume by water
 Dilute milk at 35-37oC
 Put the milk in a clean bucket
 Washing hands with an antiseptics
 Dip fingers into the fresh milk
 Lower the calf’s head into the bucket to suck the fingers.
 Gently lower the calf’s head as it sucks the fingers to drink the milk
 Carefully and slowly withdraw the fingers as the calf continues to drink the milk.
 Repeat the procedure every other day till the calf automatically comes and drinks on its own
