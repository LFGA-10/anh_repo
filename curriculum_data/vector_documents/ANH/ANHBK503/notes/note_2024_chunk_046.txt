Nosema (only in A. mellifera)
Nosema is a fungal disease that affects honey bees and other pollinators,
caused by the microsporidian parasites Nosema apis and Nosema ceranae. It
can be detected by microscope. The disease often occurs after a long rest
period. If sufficient pollen can be collected the disease has little effect.
Nosema spores are microscopic and cannot be seen with the naked eye. They
infect the midgut (intestinal tract) of adult honey bees, where they multiply and
cause damage to the digestive system. Nosema-infected bees may exhibit
symptoms such as dysentery (diarrhoea), weakened flight capabilities, reduced
lifespan, and overall colony decline.
3.1.2 Treatment of bee’s parasitic diseases method
➢ Identification of drugs for bees’ treatment
• Fumagilin-B for Nosema or Acetic in Fumigation
• Apistan ™ STRIPS for varroa mites
• APIGUARD for varroa mite
• Menthol crystals provide a fumigant action that kills tracheal mites
without harming the bees at certain temperatures. The crystals start
to evaporate about 70°F and will melt to a liquid at about 102-
105°F.
• Predatory Mites: Predatory mites such as Stratiolaelaps scimitus
(formerly known as Hypoaspis miles) and Neoseiulus californicus
can be introduced into honey bee colonies to prey on Varroa mites
and other pests. These mites establish themselves in the hive
environment and help control mite populations without the use of
chemicals.
