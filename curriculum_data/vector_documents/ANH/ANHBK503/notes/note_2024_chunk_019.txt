Swarming preparation: when the new queen cells are nearly capped,
indicating that the new queens are about to emerge, the old queen and a
portion of the worker bees begin to prepare for swarming. The workers fill the
honeycomb cells with honey to sustain them during the swarming process.
Swarm Departure: Once the new queen bees have emerged and matured, the
old queen and a large portion of the worker bees leave the hive in a large group,
known as the swarm. The swarm typically exits the hive and gathers nearby,
often clustering on a tree branch or other object.
Cluster Formation: The swarm forms a temporary cluster, with the queen
protected at the center. Scout bees are dispatched to search for a suitable new
hive site while the swarm waits. During this time, the swarm may remain in
place for several hours to a few days, depending on how quickly the scouts find
a suitable location.
Selection of New Hive Site: Scout bees search for potential new hive sites,
evaluating factors such as cavity size, insulation, entrance size, and location.
Once a suitable site is found, the scouts return to the swarm and communicate
the location and quality of the site through dancing and pheromone release.
Move to New Hive Site: Once a consensus is reached among the scout bees
regarding the new hive site, the swarm departs from its temporary location and
flies to the chosen site. The bees begin constructing comb and establishing a
new colony in their new home.
