• Screening: Screen incoming bees and equipment for signs of parasitic
infestations, diseases, or abnormal behavior before introducing them to
existing colonies.
• Controlled Access: Limit access to apiaries and hive sites to authorized
personnel and implement visitor protocols to prevent the inadvertent
spread of pests and diseases.
Offensive Measures:
• Monitoring: Regularly monitor honey bee colonies for signs of parasitic
infestations, diseases, and abnormal behavior through visual
inspections, sampling, and diagnostic testing.
• Early Detection: Detect parasitic diseases and pest infestations early to
implement timely intervention and prevent their spread to other colonies
or apiaries.

Defensive Measures:
• Selective Breeding: Select and breed honey bee stocks with traits for
resistance or tolerance to specific parasitic pests and diseases, such as
Varroa mites and Nosema.

3.2 Identify bee infectious disease
3.2.1. Bacterial diseases
1. American foulbrood
American foulbrood (AFB) is a fatal bacterial disease of honey bee brood caused
by the spore forming bacterium Paenibacillus larvae. It is not a stress related
disease and can infect the strongest to the weakest colony in an apiary.
Infected brood usually die at the pre-pupal or pupal stage. Heavy infections can
affect most of the brood, severely weakening the colony and eventually killing
it. The disease is not able to be cured, meaning that destruction of infected
colonies and hives or irradiation of infected material is the only way to manage
AFB. The bacteria infect the larva and the disease is transferred through
infected frames and hives, but also through honey.
