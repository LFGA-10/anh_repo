3.3. Identify and control bee predators
Wax moth
Symptoms:
• Grey caterpillars 0.5 to 2.5 cm long can be seen scrawling over the top
bars when you remove the inner lid.
• Combs are affected and the spaces between the combs are covered in
spider webs.
• Whole combs are eaten up and changed into a grey-black mass spun
together with webs.
Description:
This pest occurs in weak colonies which cannot occupy all the frames, and in
supplies of unused frames with comb. There are two kinds of wax moth, the
larger and the smaller wax moth, 2 and 1 cm long respectively and both grey.
They lay their eggs in cracks and grooves. The eggs hatch into caterpillars
which move to the frames.
Treatment:
➢ Always remove frames from the colony which cannot be occupied by bees
and store these in a protected place.
➢ Transfer lightly affected frames to the strong colonies. The bees will clean
them and repair them.
➢ Put the affected frames in a closed area and treat them with glacial acetic
acid or smoke of burning sulfur.
➢ Store frames with good comb in an airtight container or box which you
disinfect from time to time as mentioned above. Watch out for mould.
