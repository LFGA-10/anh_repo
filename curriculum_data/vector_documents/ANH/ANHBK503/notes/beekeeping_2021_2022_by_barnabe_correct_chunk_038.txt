Reproduction of the Queen
The queen is usually the only female which lays eggs, because a queen
produces the most eggs in the first year of her life, the best queens are not
more than 2 years old.

Because she must fly some distance from her colony to mate, she first circles
the hive to orient herself to its location. The queen mates, usually in the
afternoon, with seven (7) to fifteen (15) drones at an altitude above 6m.
Drones are able to find and recognize the queen by her chemical odor
(pheromone).
After mating, the Queen returns to the hive and begins laying eggs in about 48
hours. Once mated, queens may lay up to 2,000 eggs per day.
The bad weather delays the queen’s mating flight, if the delay achieves more
than 20 days; the queen loses the ability to mate and will only be able to lay
unfertilized eggs, which result in drones.
She releases (let go) several sperm from the spermatheca each time she lays an
egg destined to become either a worker or queen.
If her egg is laid in a larger drone-sized cell, she normally does not release
sperm, and the resulting individual becomes a drone.
