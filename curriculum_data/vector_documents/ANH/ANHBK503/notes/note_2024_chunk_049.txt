Paenibacillus larvae occurs in two forms: vegetative (rod-shaped bacterial cells)
and spores. Only the spore stage is infectious to honey bees. Larvae less than
53 hours old become infected by swallowing spores present in their food. Older
larvae are not susceptible. The spores germinate into the vegetative stage soon
after they enter the larval gut and continue to multiply until larval death.
New spores form after the larva dies. Death typically occurs after the cell is
capped, during the last 2 days of the larval stage or first 2 days of the pupal
stage.
Symptoms:
✓ The bees die in the larval or pupal stage (capped brood).
✓ The cell capping of the affected larvae is indented.
✓ The brood nest is irregular.
✓ In the affected cells you will find a sticky mass, which forms threads
when you remove it from the cell with a matchstick.
✓ There is a smell of glue (glutton) in the hive.
✓ Affected larvae are dark brown to black.
