b. Swarming control
Check for signs of swarming during hive inspections and at other times. If
swarm cells are along the bottoms of frames, remove the swarm cells and
correct the conditions stimulating swarming.

Supersedure swarms contain one or more virgin queens. The former queen is
left behind to be replaced by a queen still developing.
You can prevent supersedure swarming by maintaining a vigorous queen in the
colony. This is usually assured by requeening the colony every other year.
4.1.5. Absconding
Absconding differs from swarming in that colonies which abscond do not leave
behind a young queen works. The absconding colonies take everything with
them and simply leave to establish new nests elsewhere.
Absconding has two forms: disturbance-induced and resource-induced.
Occasionally an entire colony leaves the hive, abandoning the brood nest.
In this event no queen cells have been built and it is therefore not a case of
social reproduction.
