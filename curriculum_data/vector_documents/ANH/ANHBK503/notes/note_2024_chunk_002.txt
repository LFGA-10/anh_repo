LEARNING UNIT 1: INSTALL AN APIARY
Apiary is place where bees are kept, or a collection of beehives while
Apiculture is technical term for beekeeping.
1.1 Selection an apiary site
The selection of a site for an apiary involves careful consideration of
various factors to ensure the well-being of the honeybees and the
productivity of the hive.
Apiary site selection criteria Environment:
➢ Sources of water: Bees need a source of fresh water so they can regulate
hive temperature, liquefy crystallized honey, and raise brood. You can
provide a tank or pan of water with a floating board if a water supply is
not available within 1⁄4 miles of the hives. The water source does not
need to be “pure.”.
➢ Ecological Condition:
❖ Sun: Bees are less irritable and easier to handle when located in
the open where they can get plenty of sunshine. Prefer to face the
beehive south or southeast.
Either, the ideal placement of beehives with regard to sunlight
depends on various factors, and there is no one-size-fits-all answer
as different climates and conditions may warrant different
considerations.
❖ Rain: You should avoid apiary locations in damp bottom land
since excess moisture and less sunshine retard the flight of the
bees and encourage development of bee diseases.
❖ Wind: The apiary is best situated near natural wind protection
such as hills, buildings, or evergreens.
❖ Humidity: An apiary must be on dry ground and good air
drainage.
