4.1.4. Swarming and absconding
a. Swarming
Swarm cells are built during the period when the brood nest develops rapidly.
The bees become less active, fewer bees fly out to forage, and no more comb is
built. The colony is preparing to divide itself, and may do this even several
times unless you intervene. A good beekeeper follows the development of the
colony and tries to prevent swarming in time, although this will certainly not
always succeed.
In general, the yellow bee is enough to make swarming. It can be natural or
artificial.
Before swarming the queen lays eggs and gives up the eggs. It is what is called
‘fever of swarming’. The workers are agitated forage less, build the royal cells
and fill their basket.
