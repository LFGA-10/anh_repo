➢ Colony merging/Strengthening
This process engages with joining two colonies together. This may be to save
the bees in a colony, which is hopelessly queen less or to cull a queen with
undesirable traits or simply to reduce stocks.
For uniting to be successful therefore, it must be done in such a way as to
disguise the odour, and reduce the risk of putting the bees on the defensive.
The simplest way is to place one colony on top of another separated only by
newspaper. The bees will chew through the paper and unite, but this will
probably take at least twenty-four hours.
➢ Replacement of the queen/Re-queening
Most beekeepers prefer to replace the queen before she begins to fail. This can
be a yearly process. Requeening is relatively inexpensive, especially when you
consider the production loss and generally weakened colony due to a failing
queen.

2.4.2 Feeding techniques
➢ Water distribution
A simple way to give bees water is just to fill your bird bath with stones. If
you already have a bird bath, you can make it attractive to bees by adding
these additional landing areas. You can provide a tank or pan of water with
a floating board or crushed rock for the bees to land on.
