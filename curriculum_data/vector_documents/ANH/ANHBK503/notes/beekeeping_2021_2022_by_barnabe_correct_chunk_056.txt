✓ Prepare your new hive first(clean and rub it with some bee wax or propolis
so that it smells familiar for the bees).Put it to the overcrowded hive
✓ Use the smoker alight nearby in case you need it
✓ Choose a big healthy colony to divide and check it has brood,eggs, pollen
and honey.
✓ Select a comb with queen cells, remove it from the hive and break all the
queen cells except the biggest capped two or you need two just in case one
gets damaged.
✓ Now transfer the comb with the 2 queen cells into the new hive.
✓ Also transfer one or two other the combs with a lot of sealed brood and a
little unsealed brood, (more brood means adult bees will emerge very quickly
in new hive).
✓ Also transfer one or two combs of food comb with lots of sealed honey and
pollen.
✓ Make a division with combs as long as the new colony has female work eggs.
From the very young larvae they will be able to make new queen cells within
a few days and raise a new queen.
✓ Include bees on all the combs you transfer and brush or shake in bees from
2 or 3 other combs as well.
✓ Check very thoroughly that you do not have the old queen on the combs.
(she must remain undisturbed in the old hive or mother colony).
✓ These bees will become a new colony. Most of adult bees remain in the old
hive and continue to make honey.
✓ Wait until dark then move the new hive to site least 2 km from the old site,
if you don’t have enough place, put the new hive at 1m away the old
location.
