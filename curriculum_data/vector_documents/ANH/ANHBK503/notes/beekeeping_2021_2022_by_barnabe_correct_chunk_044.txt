The eggs hatch after three days; the larval stage lasts for about 5 days. During
this period the nurse bees provide the larvae in the open cells regularly with
small quantities of food.
After this the bees close the brood cell with a porous wax capping. This is
called sealed or capped brood. The larva inside spins a cocoon expels its
excreta and then becomes a pre-pupa and pupa.
The capped phase of the worker brood lasts about 12 days.

Then the bee which has developed from the pupa gnaws away the wax capping
and appears on the comb. The newly emerged light-grey bees are easy to
recognize.
The drones take the longest to develop. The open brood stage lasts about 7
days and the capped brood stage about 15-16 days.
The young queens, however, are ready to emerge within 7 days. It is important
for the beekeeper to know that after a hive has lost its queen he can expect the
birth of a young queen after about 13 days.
Notice: after hatching, all brood (larva of queen, worker and drones) is fed on
royal jelly form 2 days.
After the only queen is continuously fed on royal jelly up death and workers
and drones are fed on honey, pollen and water.
This difference in feeding results the different in morphological development of
those 3 castes.
