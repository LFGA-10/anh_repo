4. Gloves
Beginners who fear being stung should wear canvas or leather gloves. Many
experienced bee-keepers find gloves cumbersome and decide to risk a few
stings for the sake of easier handling. Form-fitting gloves (such as those
suitable for lab work or household chores) reduce stings and sticky fingers
from honey and propolis
➢ Colony manipulation tools
1. Smoker
It is used to give smoke to the bees for easy handling. The smoker consists of a
metal fire pot and grate with bellows attached. The size of the smoker is a
matter of individual preference. The 4 x 7 inch size is probably the most widely
used.
To produce large quantities of cool, thick smoke, coals must be above the grate
and unburned materials must be above the coals. Suitable smoker fuels
include burlap, corn cobs, wood shavings, pine needles, cardboard, punk
wood, bark, sumac bobs, cotton rags, dry leaves, and bailer twine. An
alternative liquid smoke is available that you mix with water and spray onto
the bees with a mister-type applicator.
