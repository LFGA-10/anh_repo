➢ Administration techniques of drugs
• Drug solution: Drug solutions are commonly administered to honey
bee colonies by mixing the medication with sugar syrup or water and
feeding it to the bees.
• Fumigation: Fumigation involves exposing honey bee colonies
to vaporized or aerosolized medications to treat pests and
diseases.
47

Choose an appropriate fumigant based on the target pest or
disease and follow the manufacturer's guidelines for application.
Use specialized fumigation equipment, such as vaporizers or
foggers, to disperse the medication evenly throughout the hive.
Seal the hive to contain the fumigant and allow sufficient
exposure time for effective treatment.
• Drugs added in feeds: Some medications can be directly added
to bee feeds, such as pollen patties or supplemental feeds, to
deliver treatment to honey bee colonies.
3.1.3 Prevention of bee’s parasitic diseases method
General Hygienic Measures:
• Hive Maintenance: Regularly inspect and maintain beehives to remove
debris, old comb, and hive equipment that may harbor pests and
pathogens.
• Hygienic Practices: Practice good beekeeping hygiene, such as wearing
clean protective gear, using clean tools and equipment, and washing
hands before handling bees or hive components.
• Hive Placement: Choose suitable hive locations away from sources of
contamination, such as pesticides, pollutants, and sources of stagnant
water.
2. Biosecurity Measures:
• Quarantine: Quarantine new bee colonies, equipment, and beeswax to
prevent the introduction of pests and diseases into established apiaries.
• Screening: Screen incoming bees and equipment for signs of parasitic
infestations, diseases, or abnormal behavior before introducing them to
existing colonies.
