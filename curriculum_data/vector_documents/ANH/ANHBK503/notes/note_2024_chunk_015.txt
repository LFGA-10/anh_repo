2.1.2.3 Apis Mellifera Scutellata and Unicolor (Inyumbo)
Native to central and eastern Africa respectively: both of these species have a
very pronounced, defensive behaviour and are prone to swarming and
absconding (meaning: the entire colony leaves the old hive).
2.1.2.4 Apis Mellifera Ligustica
Known as the Italian bee. This race is very adaptable to different environments.

2.1.3 Development of the Bees/Life cycle
The eggs hatch after three days; the larval stage lasts for about 5 days. During
this period the nurse bees provide the larvae in the open cells regularly with
small quantities of food. After this the bees close the brood cell with a porous
wax capping. This is called sealed or capped brood. The larva inside spins a
cocoon expels its excreta and then becomes a pre-pupa and pupa. The capped
phase of the worker brood lasts about 12 days.
Then the bee which has developed from the pupa gnaws away the wax capping
and appears on the comb. The newly emerged light-grey bees are easy to
recognize. The drones take the longest to develop.
The open brood stage lasts about 7-8 days and the capped brood stage about
15-16 days. The young queens, however, are ready to emerge within 7 days. It
is important for the beekeeper to know that after a hive has lost its queen, he
can expect the birth of a young queen after about 13 days.
2.1.4 Selection criteria of the starter colony
For a successful beekeeper, beekeeping production depends on the selection of
better performing honey bee colonies. Thus, selection aims and criteria are
important tools in bees breeding programme for a beekeeper in an apiary.
