Symptoms:

✓ The workers drag out the dead larvae.
Treatment:
✓ Remove the severely affected frames and melt them down. Provide frames
from unaffected colonies.
✓ Lock up the queen in a queen cage in the hive for 5 to 10 days so that
the workers have a chance to remove all the diseased larvae. When this
has been done release her again.
✓ Chemical: (in combination with the above) treat with an antibiotic
(tetramycine, sulfathiazole etc.) mixed with sugar solution (sugar/water =
1:1).
3.2.2. Viral diseases
1. Sac Brood Virus
Description:
This disease is caused by a virus Iflavirus genus. In A.mellifera the disease is
not serious, but in A.cerana a certain strain causes a hazard to many colonies.
This strain is called “Thai sacbrood virus”. There is a genetic base for this
disease. Initially only few colonies are resistant, but new generations show
more resistance.
Symptoms:
 In general, quite similar to European foul brood, but without a sour
smell.
 Infected larvae fail to pupate correctly and die, often appearing stretched
out in their cells. They eventually form a sac-like structure filled with
fluid.
 Larvae die in an early stage, leaving a watery sac at the bottom of the
cells.
 The watery sac turns into leather like scales.
Treatment:
➢ Continue beekeeping with new colonies that show no disease. Take new
queens from these colonies only
