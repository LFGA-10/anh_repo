Beekeepers may select specific colonies known for their prolific production of
royal jelly. These colonies may have a high proportion of queen cells and
strong, healthy worker bees capable of producing abundant royal jelly.
Royal jelly is harvested when the queen larvae are at the appropriate stage of
development, typically between 3 to 4 days old. At this stage, the larvae are
floating in a pool of royal jelly within their queen cells.

There are several techniques for harvesting royal jelly, but one common method
involves using specialized tools called royal jelly grafting tools or cups. These
tools are used to carefully scoop royal jelly from the queen cells without
damaging the larvae or disrupting the colony.
Beekeepers gently scoop royal jelly from the queen cells using a royal jelly
grafting tool and transfer it to collection containers. The collected royal jelly is
then carefully stored in sterile containers or vials for further processing and
storage.
Once harvested, royal jelly may undergo additional processing, such as
filtration and pasteurization, to remove impurities and ensure food safety. It is
then stored in airtight containers in a cool, dark environment to preserve its
freshness and quality.
4. Bee Venom
Bee Venom is extracted from Honeybees using low voltage electrical
stimulation. Beekeepers use a device called a bee sting pad. This device
consists of a surface with a mild electric current that encourages bees to sting
it. When a bee stings the collector, the electric current triggers the bee's stinger
to pump venom into a collection chamber. The venom is usually dried or freeze-
dried to preserve its potency and purity.
