2. Cloudy wing virus
Cloudy Wing Virus (CWV) is a viral pathogen that affects honeybees,
specifically Apis mellifera. It is one of several viruses that can compromise the
health of bee colonies, contributing to the decline in bee populations
CWV is commonly diagnosed in hives with varroa mite infestations. Although
researchers don’t have conclusive research, they hypothesize that these mites
are a vector for the infection.
Symptoms:
➢ Bees crawling or paralyzed in front of the hive.
➢ Cloudy, opaque wings.
➢ Foul-smelling dysentery.
➢ Large numbers of dead bees outside the hive.
➢ Deformed, shriveled wings.
Transmissions:
➢ Varroa Mites: The Varroa destructor mite is a primary vector for many
bee viruses, including CWV. The mites transmit the virus while feeding
on the hemolymph (blood) of bees.
➢ Horizontal Transmission: The virus can spread within a colony from
bee to bee, particularly through feeding and grooming behaviors.
➢ Vertical Transmission: The virus can also be passed from queen to
offspring.
Control and treatment:
➢ Since Varroa mites are a major vector for CWV, controlling mite
populations is crucial.
➢ Introducing a new queen from a resistant stock can improve colony
health and resilience.
