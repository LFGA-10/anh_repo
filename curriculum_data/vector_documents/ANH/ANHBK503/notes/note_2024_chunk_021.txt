2.2.6 Absconding phenomenon
Absconding differs from swarming in that colonies which abscond do not leave
behind a young queen works. The absconding colonies take everything with
them and simply leave to establish new nests elsewhere. Absconding has two
forms: disturbance-induced and resource-induced. Occasionally an entire
colony leaves the hive, abandoning the brood nest. In this event no queen cells
have been built and it is therefore not a case of social reproduction.
Possible reasons for this are:
1. Shortage of food (the cluster of bees is then, incorrectly, called a hunger
swarm.), or reduction in the honey flow. Apis mellifera adasonii will move
to find enough food in the mountain areas and the plains, and the
reverse, every year (seasonal migration).
2. Disturbance of the colony, either by ants, wasps, termites or even the
beekeeper.
