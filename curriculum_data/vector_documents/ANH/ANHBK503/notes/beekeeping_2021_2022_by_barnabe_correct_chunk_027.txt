(iv)Rain: Place the hives at a slight angle so that the flight entrance is 1 cm
lower than the rear of the bottom board and the rainwater cannot run into the
hive. The hive cover must be waterproof.
(v)Cattle/game: Make sure that cattle or large game cannot walk around the
hives. Fence the bee site if necessary.
(vi) Raccoons: If raccoons cause too much trouble, it is advisable to suspend
the hives from the branches of a tree or a from a home-made pole construction
with wire. The hives should hang about 1 m above the ground.
(vii) Toads: In the humid tropics toads are notorious bee-eaters. In this case
always place the hives at least 50 cm above the ground.

(viii) Ants, termites: In many areas ants are a great threat to bees, and care
must be taken to protect the bees from them. One solution is to suspend the
hives with wires. A second solution is to use hive stands which are protected
from ants and termites by grease. Fix a collar of zinc or aluminium to each leg
of the stand and grease the underside of these collars once a year with old
crankcase oil.
2.3. Construct an apiary
The shelter of the hives will be well arranged. On recommends for 5 hives, a
shelter of the following size is 4m length, 2m height and 2m board.
The three sides are closed by walls in various materials (bricks, wood, beaten
ground etc.). Wood is recommended because it allows ventilation.
The shelter protects the bees against the bad weather and facilitates the
beekeeper to easily work under the shade.
