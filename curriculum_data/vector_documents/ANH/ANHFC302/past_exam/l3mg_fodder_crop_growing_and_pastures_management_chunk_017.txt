L.U:1 Cultivate fodder crops; L.O 1.5. Plant fodder crops
Answer
A) Planting by seed (Sowing method)
Broadcasting method
(Fodder Crops and Pasture Management) Page 14 of 18 – Marking Guides

Spread the seeds by hand, taking care to ensure uniform distribution;
Divided the area into equal parts and the seeds divided into similar equal
parts; Each portion of seed is broadcasted into one area.
Wind is a factor to be considered especially with light seeds which may be
blown over some distance. Mixing the seeds with materials such as
sawdust, sand and fertilizers helps distribute the seed more evenly.
All grasses and small seeded legumes are planted at a depth of 1.2-1.5 cm.
B) Planting by vegetative parts
This is a method by which plants reproduce themselves from part that is
not associated with the reproductive organs.
Most of them have ample storage organs that serve as reserves when
conditions are unfavorable.
As soon as the conditions allow, the plants rejuvenate and start growing a
fresh.
Procedures:
✓ Mature stems bearing three nodes are cut;
✓ Cuttings are planted in furrows or surface broadcast and covered with
soil;
✓ The two nodes buried and one left above ground;
✓ The spacing is 0.7 X 0.3 m inter-row and for runner grasses 2 X 2m
✓ Planting one cane/root split in holes 15-30 cm deep.
C) Planting by seedlings.
Seedling in the nursery for browse trees and shrubs, it is advisable to
raise seedlings some two months ahead of the rainy season using plastic
bags in a nursery house.
The procedures are:
Prepare a mixture of fine sand, forest soil and farmyard manure.
Mix well and moisten by sprinkling with water and filling the plastic pots.
Arrange the pots under a nursery shed and plant viable seeds (two seeds per
hole if seed is not limiting)
