▪ Principle: Conserving forage by drying it to a low
moisture content (below 15-20%) to inhibit the

o Method 2: Silage making (2 marks):
▪ Principle: Conserving forage through anaerobic
fermentation. The forage is chopped and stored in
airtight conditions (e.g., a silo or pit), where natural
bacteria produce acids that preserve it. (1 mark)
o (Alternative method: Oversowing/Reseeding can also
be accepted if explained as a way to improve pasture
quality for dry season grazing, but hay/silage are
more direct conservation methods).
Level: Analyzing
3. Question: Compare and contrast natural
pasture and artificial (planted) pasture in terms of their
establishment, productivity, and management requirements.
Marking Guide (Total: 9 marks)
o Establishment (3 marks):
▪ Natural: Establishes spontaneously without sowing
by humans. (1 mark)
▪ Artificial: Requires deliberate land preparation, seed
selection, and planting. (2 marks)
o Productivity (3 marks):
▪ Natural: Generally lower forage biomass and
variable quality. (1 mark)
▪ Artificial: Higher and more predictable forage
biomass, better quality, especially if legumes are
included. (2 marks)
o Management (3 marks):
▪ Natural: Management focuses on controlling
bushes/weeds, providing water, and over-sowing. (1
mark)
