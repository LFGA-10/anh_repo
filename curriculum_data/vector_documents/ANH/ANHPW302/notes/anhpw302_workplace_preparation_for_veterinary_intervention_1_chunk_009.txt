Cephalosporins

Cephalosprins have commonly been used in veterinary medicine for many infections, including pyoderma, urinary tract infection, pneumonia, soft-tissue infection, osteomyelitis, and pre- and postsurgical use.

Microbial susceptibility:

First Generation: The first generation drugs are effective against almost all gram-positive bacteria, except Enterococcus.

Second Generation.  In general, these drugs have greater activity against many gram-negative bacteria that are resistant to the first-generation drugs.

Third Generation.  This group of antibiotics has more activity against gram-negative bacteria than the earlier generations of cephalosporins.

Fourth Generation.   Recently, there has been designation of a fourth-generation of cephalosporins. The use of these has not been reported in veterinary medicine except for some experimental studies.

Aminoglycosides

Aminoglucosides are powerful group of antimicrobials used in veterinary medicine to combat a variety of serious bacterial infections.

Microbial susceptibility: Aminoglucosides are highly effective against many oxygen-dependant (aerobic) bacteria but are ineffective against most anaerobic bacteria.
