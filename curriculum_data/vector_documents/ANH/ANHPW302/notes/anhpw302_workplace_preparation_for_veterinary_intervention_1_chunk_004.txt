Chemical name: The chemical names are the scientific names, based on the molecular structure of the drug. Chemical names are typically very long and too complex to be commonly used in referring to a drug in speech or in prose documents.

Eg: 1-(isopropylamino)-3-(1-naphthyloxy) propan-2-ol" is a chemical name for propranolol.

Generic/nonproprietary: is more concise name given to the specific chemical compound. The nonproprietary names are usually listed as active ingredient on drug labels and can be found on the drug label for many different drug manufacturers.

 Example: Amoxicillin

Trade name: is a unique name a manufacturer gives its particularly brand of drug, because the trade names are proper nouns they are capitalized. The names are followed by the marks ®or ™ to signify that the trade name is registered trademark and cannot be legally used by other manufacturers.

Example: Amoxi-Drop®, Biomox®, Robamox-V®

Topic 3: Composition of a drug

A form of a drug is traditionally composed by: active ingredient, excipient adjuvant and additives.

Active ingredient: Any substance or combination of substances used in a finished pharmaceutical product (FPP), intended to furnish pharmacological  activity or to  otherwise have direct effect in the diagnosis, cure, mitigation, treatment or  prevention of disease, or to have direct effect  in restoring, correcting or modifying physiological functions in human beings."
