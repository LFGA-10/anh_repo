E. Ruminatoric and antibloat  medication

			i. 	Ruminotorics

Agents and mixtures that promote forestomach function (fermentation and motility) are known as ruminotorics.

Cholinergicssuch as neostigmine or bethanechol transiently increase the frequency but not the strength of contractions in rumen atony.Since they do not provide the synchronized contractions and relaxations of theforestomachs, their value for the movement of ingesta is limited. They are administered SC.

Metoclopropamide: experimentally has been reported to increase ruminoreticular motility and abomasal contractions. Opiate antagonists such as naloxone stimulate extrinsic contractions in endotoxin-induced ruminal stasis when administered parenterally.

Ruminal fluid transfer.Oral inoculation of viable rumen bacteria and protozoa is the most effective means of restoring rumen function following correction of the primary cause of stasis.

			ii. 	Bloat therapy

Ruminal bloat or tympany is the accumulation of excess gas in the rumen as a result of impaired elimination, not excess production. Ruminal gasses may be in the free form or, more commonly, entrapped in froth.

Antibloat agents alter the surface tension of froth and break up the bubbles which contain entrapped gases:

Poloxalene (25–50 g for cattle).

Polymerized methyl silicone (3% emulsion; 30–60 mL in cattle; 7–15 mL in sheep).

Vegetable oils (soybean, peanut, or sunflower oil); 60 mL in cattle; 10–15 mL in sheep.
