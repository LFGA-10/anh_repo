1.3: Identify and select veterinary drugs acting on different systems
The Anatomical Therapeutic Chemical (ATC) Classification System is used for the classification of drugs. This
pharmaceutical coding system divides drugs into different groups according to the organ or system on
which they act and/or their therapeutic and chemical characteristics.
Topic 1: Drug acting on gastro-intestinal tract
A. Emetics drugs
Emesis(vomiting) is a normal, protective mechanism designed to remove substances the body perceives as
toxic. An emetic is that produces vomiting with the intent of removing an ingested toxic substance before it
can be absorbed by the body.
i. Central acting emetics
 Apomorphine HCl
It is also effective IM, SQ, or via conjunctival drops. Apomorphine administration should notbe repeated
because the vomiting center is depressed after the initialchemoreceptor trigger zone(CRTZ) stimulation.
 Xylazine
Xylazineinduces vomiting in cats following IV or IM administration. Onset of vomiting occurs in 2–5 minutes
and may be followed by mild sedation for 30–90 minutes.
ii. Peripheral acting emetics
Peripheral acting emetics stimulate sympathetic and vagal afferent receptors in pharynx and stomach to
induce vomiting.
 Sodium chloride
It is administered as crystals of table salt. It is the most effective household emetic in dogs.
 Syrup of Ipecac
It contains emetine alkaloid and is the recommended household emetic for children. It is less reliable in
dogs and cats but may induce vomiting in 15–30 minutes.
