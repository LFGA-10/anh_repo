D. Laxatives and purgatives

Laxatives promote evacuation of the bowel through stimulation of fluid and electrolyte transport and increases in propulsive motility.

Hyperosmotic laxatives:

Lactulose is the most effective agent in this group. The organic acids producedfrom lactulose fermentation stimulate colonic fluid secretion and propulsive motility.

Polyethylene glycol: are isotonic mixtures of polyethylene glycol, sodium sulfate, sodium bicarbonate, sodium chloride, and potassium chloride.

Magnesium sulfate: Epsom salts or magnesium hydroxide.

Bulk laxatives

Bulk laxatives comprise poorly digestible polysaccharides which absorb water and increase fecal bulk which stimulates large bowel.

Lubricants and surfactants:  Mineral oil (liquid petrolatum) and white petrolatum lubricate and soften fecal mass.

Emollient laxatives: Emollient laxatives are anionic detergents that increase the miscibility of water and lipid in digesta, thus enhancing lipid absorption and impairing water absorption.

Example: Dioctyl sodium,sulfosuccinate and dioctyl calcium sulfosuccinate.

Irritant laxatives

Irritant cathartics are derived from plants and are activated in the GI tract to release irritant derivatives which activate myenteric neurons and smooth muscle to increase gut motility. They are used primarily in non-ruminant large animals.

 Example: Anthraquinone (emodin).
