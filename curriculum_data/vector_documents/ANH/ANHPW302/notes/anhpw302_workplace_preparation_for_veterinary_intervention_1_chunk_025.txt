Hydralazine

Hydralazine is a phthalazine derivative vasodilating agent. Hydralazine directly relaxes arteriolar smooth muscle with little effect on the venous system.

Therapeutic uses: Hydralazine, in combination with furosemide, is especially useful for dogs with mitral insufficiency and severe pulmonary edema by rapidly reducing arterial resistance (afterload). Hydralazine has also been used in the treatment of myocardial failure and hypertension.

Amlodipine

Amlodipine besylate is dihydropyridine Ca2+channel blocker.

Therapeutic uses: Amlodipine is the drug of first choice for systemic arterial hypertension in cats. It can be a useful adjunct therapeutic agent in hypertensive dogs.

Nitrates

Commonly used nitrates include sodium nitroprusside (for IV infusion), nitroglycerinetopical ointment (2%), and oral isosorbidedinitrate.

C. Diuretics

Diuretic therapy is indicated to control edema and effusions that occur in chronic heart failure. Excessive use of diuretics in heart failure exacerbates neurohormonal activation.

Furosemide: This loop-diuretic is used almost exclusively for animals with cardiogenic edema or effusion.

Adverse effects are usually related to excessive fluid and/or electrolyte losses.

Spironolactone. This K+ sparing diuretic may be a useful adjunct therapy for chronic refractory heart failure, although it appears to have little diuretic effect in normal dogs.

Adverse effects relate to excess K+ retention and GI disturbances.

Thiazide diuretics. Chlorothiazide or hydrochlorothiazide is occasionally used in combination with furosemide and other therapy for refractory CHF.
