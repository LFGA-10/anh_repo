Iodine compounds and iodophors

Iodine compound and Iodophors are most commonly used as topical antiseptics before surgery procedure or for disinfection of tissues. Iodine and high concentrations of Iodophors are bactericidal, virusidal, protozoacidal and fungicidal.

Chlorhexidine

Chlorhexidine, is a member of a class of antiseptic known as Biguanides, is one of the most commonly used disinfectants and antiseptics compounds in veterinary medicine. It has virusidal, bactericidal (both grampositive and gram negative), and fungicidal properties.

Quaternary ammonium compounds

Quaternary ammonium compounds or “Quats” are used in veterinary medicine to disinfect the surfaces of inanimate objects. Quaternary ammonium compounds are effective against gram-positive bacteria,but they are ineffective  against bacterial spores and have poor efficacy against fungi and gram-negative bacteria.

 Example: Benzalkonium chloride.

Peroxides and oxidants

 Are the drugs that kill or inhibit the growth of micro-organism or microbes such as bacteria, protozoa and fungi. The term antibiotic is often used interchangeably with the term antimicrobial.

Penicillins

Penicillin remains a popular antibiotic and is still the drug of choice for initial treatment of many infections in horses and cattle. It has little usefulness in small animals because of the high incidence of resistance.
