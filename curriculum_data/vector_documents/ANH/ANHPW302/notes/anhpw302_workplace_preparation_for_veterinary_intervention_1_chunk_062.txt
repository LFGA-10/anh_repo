Topic 5: Disposal techniques and methods

The safe disposal and subsequent destruction of clinical waste is a key step in the reduction of illness or injury through contact with this potentially hazardous material, and in the prevention of environmental contamination. There are three common methods of waste disposal; these include burning, burying and recycling.

 Burning or Incineration

Incineration is the process that converts combustible materials into noncombustible ash, achieving a reduction of 90% by volume or 75% by weight. The product gases are vented into the atmosphere, and the treatment residue may be disposed of in a landfill.  Burying or Landfill

It is acceptable to dispose of specific categories of waste in a properly managed landfill provided there are procedures in place to protect workers from contact with the waste. Studies have shown that bacteria and viruses in a landfill are significantly reduced in number by processes such as thermal inactivation and adsorption of organic material in the solid waste.   Waste recycling

Some wastes may be collected and sent to recycling centers and they are reused; this may be useful because the virgin resources are saved, the landfill spaces are reduced and the quantity of CO2 resulting from combustion is reduced.
