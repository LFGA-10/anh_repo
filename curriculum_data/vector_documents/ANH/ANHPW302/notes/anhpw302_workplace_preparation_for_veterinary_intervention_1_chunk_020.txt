Apomorphine HCl

Xylazineinduces vomiting in cats following IV or IM administration. Onset of vomiting occurs in 2–5 minutes and may be followed by mild sedation for 30–90 minutes.

			ii. 	Peripheral acting emetics

Peripheral acting emetics stimulate sympathetic and vagal afferent receptors in pharynx and stomach to induce vomiting.

Sodium chloride

It is administered as crystals of table salt. It is the most effective household emetic in dogs.  Syrup of Ipecac

It contains emetine alkaloid and is the recommended household emetic for children. It is less reliable in dogs and cats but may induce vomiting in 15–30 minutes.

Copper sulfate (1%), zinc sulfate(1%) or hydrogen peroxide (3%) solution

They may produce emesis but the response is variable and they are seldomly used now.

B. Antiemetic drugs

Antiemetics drugs prevent or decrease vomiting.

Phenothiazines

The phenothiazine tranquilizers exert their antiemetic action by blockade of dopamine.Although they are broadspectrumantiemetics, they tend to be less effective in vomiting arising from severe inflammation of the GI tract or the inner ear.

Others examples:Acepromazine, chlorpromazine, promazine, or prochlorperazine are administered orally or IM every 6 hours to prevent vomiting in dog.

Metoclopramide: Metoclopramide has both central and peripheral antiemetic actions.
