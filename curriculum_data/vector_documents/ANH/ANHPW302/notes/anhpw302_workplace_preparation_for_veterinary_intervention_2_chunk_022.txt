B. Antiemetic drugs
Antiemetics drugs prevent or decrease vomiting.
 Phenothiazines
The phenothiazine tranquilizers exert their antiemetic action by blockade of dopamine.Although they are
broadspectrumantiemetics, they tend to be less effective in vomiting arising from severe inflammation of
the GI tract or the inner ear.
Others examples:Acepromazine, chlorpromazine, promazine, or prochlorperazine are administered orally or
IM every 6 hours to prevent vomiting in dog.
 Metoclopramide: Metoclopramide has both central and peripheral antiemetic actions.
 Butyrophenones: Droperidol and haloperidol are neuroleptic drugs which are also central acting
antiemetics.
 Antihistaminic agents: Antihistaminic agents block histaminergic and cholinergic afferents from the
vestibular organs to the vomiting center. They are useful in the prevention of motion sickness in
dogs.
 Anticholinergic agents: Anticholinergic drugs block cholinergic afferents from the GI tract to the
vomiting center. Although they are generally less active than other antiemetics when used alone,
they may be effective when combined with phenothiazines in the control of emesis arising from
severe gastroenteritis. An example of this combination is isopropamide+ prochlorperazine =
Darbazine.
