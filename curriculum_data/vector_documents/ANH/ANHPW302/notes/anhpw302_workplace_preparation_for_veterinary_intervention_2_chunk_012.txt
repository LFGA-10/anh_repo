vi. Macrolides
They are prepared as sulfate salts or as esterified salts of stearate, tartrate, estolate, or lactobionate.
Example: Erythromycin, Azithromycin, Clarithromycin, Tulathromycin, Tylosin, and Tilmicosin.

Microbial susceptibility: Macrolides are bacteriostatic by inhibiting bacterial protein synthesis. Their
antimicrobial activity is primarily against Gram(+). Tylosin and Tiamulinare effective against some Gram (–)
pathogens.

Chloramphenicol is an unusual natural compound because it contains dichloracetate and nitrobenzene
moieties as part of its structure.

viii. Sulphonamides
Sulfonamide were the first antimicrobials used on widespread basis in human and veterinary medicine,
because they have been in use for many years, many strains of bacteria have became resistant to them.
Microbial susceptibility: Sulfonamides are broad spectrum (including protozoa) and bacteriostatic.
Example: Sulfamethazine,Sulfadimethoxine,Sulfachlorpyridazine,Sulfamethoxazole,Sulfacetamideand
Sulfasalazine.
C. Antifungals
This group of antimicrobial drugs was developed to be effective against many of the fungal organism that
cause superficial mycoses.
i. Amphotericin B
Amphotericin B is effective against most of the deep mycosis; it is fungicidal against most organisms causing
systemic mycoses
ii. Griseofulvin
Griseofulvin is actively taken up by growing dermatophytes (ringworm). It binds to microtubules to inhibit
spindle formation and mitosis. It is fungistatic for dermatophytes
