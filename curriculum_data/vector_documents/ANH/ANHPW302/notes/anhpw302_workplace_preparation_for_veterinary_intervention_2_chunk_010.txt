i. Penicillins
Penicillin remains a popular antibiotic and is still the drug of choice for initial treatment of many infections
in horses and cattle. It has little usefulness in small animals because of the high incidence of resistance.

ii. Cephalosporins
Cephalosprins have commonly been used in veterinary medicine for many infections, including pyoderma,
urinary tract infection, pneumonia, soft-tissue infection, osteomyelitis, and pre- and postsurgical use.
Microbial susceptibility:
First Generation: The first generation drugs are effective against almost all gram-positive bacteria, except
Enterococcus.
Second Generation. In general, these drugs have greater activity against many gram-negative bacteria that
are resistant to the first-generation drugs.
Third Generation. This group of antibiotics has more activity against gram-negative bacteria than the
earlier generations of cephalosporins.
Fourth Generation. Recently, there has been designation of a fourth-generation of cephalosporins. The use
of these has not been reported in veterinary medicine except for some experimental studies.
