Tranquilizers and sedatives produce a relaxed state usually without producing significant analgesia (unless
the drugs specifically have analgesic properties).

C. Anesthetics
 Local anesthetics: Drugs that can be used clinically to block pain sensation from sympathetic
vasoconstrictor impulses to specific areas of the body. Basic physicochemical and pharmacodynamic
characteristics of the major aminoester and amide local anesthetics that are currently used in veterinary
practice.
 Esters: Benzocaine ,Tetracaineand Procaine
 Amides : Lidocaine, Mepivacaine, Bupivacaine and Levobupivacaine
 Other local anesthetics : Prilocaine, Proparacaine, Amethocaine, Cocaine etc.
 General anesthetics: A substance that causes general lack of feeling or awareness, pain to permit
surgery and other painful procedures.
Note: The sedatives can be used as general anesthetics when is administered in large amount.
These sedatives include: acepromazine, chlorpromazine, droperidol (Innovar-Vet) azaperone (Stresnil).
