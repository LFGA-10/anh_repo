Metoclopramide: Metoclopramide has both central and peripheral antiemetic actions.

Butyrophenones: Droperidol and haloperidol are neuroleptic drugs which are also central acting antiemetics.

Antihistaminic agents: Antihistaminic agents block histaminergic and cholinergic afferents from the vestibular organs to the vomiting center. They are useful in the prevention of motion sickness in dogs.

Anticholinergic agents: Anticholinergic drugs block cholinergic afferents from the GI tract to the vomiting center. Although they are generally less active than other antiemetics when used alone, they may be effective when combined with phenothiazines in the control of emesis arising from severe gastroenteritis. An example of this combination is isopropamide+ prochlorperazine = Darbazine.

C. Anti diarrheals

Diarrhea is defined as an increase in the frequency, volume, or fluidity of stools.

Therapy for diarrhea includes fluids, electrolyte replacement, maintenance of acid/base balance, and control of discomfort. Antiparasitic drugs or dietary therapy can also play an important role in the treatment of some types of diarrhea. Additional therapy may include intestinal protectants, motility modifiers, antimicrobials, anti-inflammatory drugs, and antitoxins.

Examples: Kaolin-pectin, Activated charcoal, Anticholinergic drugs are common ingredients in antidiarrheal preparations because they significantly decrease intestinal motility and secretions.
