ii. Expectorants
Expectorants theoretically make the bronchiole secretions less viscous but their efficacy is questionable.
 Potassium iodide is an oral saline expectorant, which causes irritation to thegastric mucosa that in turn
increases bronchiole secretion through a vagal reflex.
 Guaifenesin may stimulate the gastric mucosa and increase respiratory tract secretions via reflex.
e. Bronchodilator
Introduction
Bronchodilators are a type of medication that makes breathing easier by relaxing the muscles in the lungs
and widening the airways (bronchi).
They're often used to treat long-term conditions where the airways may become narrow and inflamed
Bronchodilators may be either:
 short-acting – used as short-term relief from sudden, unexpected attacks of breathlessness
 long-acting – used regularly, to help control breathlessness
Bronchodilators and corticosteroids
Inhaled corticosteroids are the main treatment to reduce inflammation and prevent flare-ups in asthma.
Treatment with corticosteroids and bronchodilators may require the use of separate inhalers, but
increasingly these medications are provided together in single inhalers.
