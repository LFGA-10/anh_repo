1. Furosemide: This loop-diuretic is used almost exclusively for animals with cardiogenic edema or effusion.
Adverse effects are usually related to excessive fluid and/or electrolyte losses.
2. Spironolactone. This K+ sparing diuretic may be a useful adjunct therapy for chronic refractory heart
failure, although it appears to have little diuretic effect in normal dogs.
Adverse effects relate to excess K+ retention and GI disturbances.
3. Thiazide diuretics. Chlorothiazide or hydrochlorothiazide is occasionally used in combination with
furosemide and other therapy for refractory CHF.
D. Antitussives and expectorant
i. Antitussives agents
They diminish the frequency of coughing and should not be used to suppress productive coughs. They can
be either locally acting (e.g. glycerin), or centrally acting (e.g. opiates); they can be divided into opioid and
non-opioid antitussives.
