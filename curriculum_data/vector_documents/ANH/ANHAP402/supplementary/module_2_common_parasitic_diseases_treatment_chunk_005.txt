The definitive host is the host that harbors the adult, sexual or mature stage of the parasite.

The intermediate host is the host that harbors the larval, juvenile, immature or asexual stages of the parasite.

The transport or paratenic host is a special intermediate host, in which a parasite does not undergo any development but instead remains arrested or encysted within the host’s tissues.

A reservoir host is a vertebrate host in which a parasite or disease occurs in nature and is a source of infection for human and domesticated animals.

Pathogenic role of parasites

Mechanical actions: by causing injury to the tissues and blocking the organic ducts (biliary ducts, gastro-intestinal tract, and trachea).

Voracious actions: by blood sucking and robbing the host of its food.

Toxins production: most intestinal parasites produce toxins that injure the hosts.

DESCRIPRION OF COMMON PARASITES

1.1 Description of helminthes

The word ‘helminth’ is a general term meaning ‘worm’, but there are many different types of worms. Prefixes are therefore used to designate types: platy-helminthes for flat-worms and nemathelminthes for round-worms. All helminthes are multicellular eukaryotic invertebrates with tube-like or flattened bodies exhibiting bilateral symmetry.
