In developed countries, the control of stray dogs and the use of
vaccination programmes have reduced the importance of urban rabies
and have focused attention on wildlife reservoirs.
Although virus may be transmitted through scratching and licking,
transmission usually occurs through bites. Infected animals may
excrete virus in their saliva for some time before the onset of clinical
signs.

Q21. Explain the vaccination methods. /5marks
Answer: methods of vaccination
Injection, Per os, Scarification, Spraying, Pouring
Use needles of appropriate size, based on the age of the animal, weight of
the animal, the route of product administration, and the viscosity of the
product.
✓ IM (intramuscular) – 18 or 16 g, 1 to 1 ½”
✓ SC (subcutaneous) – 18 or 16 g, ½ to 1”
✓ Oral: Into the mouth
✓ Mucosal: The administration of a vaccine to a mucosal surface such
as the digestive tract, the respiratory tract, the reproductive tract or
the inner surface of the eyelid.
✓ IN: Intranasal (into the nostrils)
✓ ID: Intradermal (within the skin)
✓ No vaccines go IV
Appropriate vaccine administration is a critical component of a successful
immunization program.
Ensure that the animal is restrained securely before injection. Use only
sharp and straight needles (discard dull, bent or burred needles).
