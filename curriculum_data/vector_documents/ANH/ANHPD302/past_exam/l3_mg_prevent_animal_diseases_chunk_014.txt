C. Rabies
C.1. Definition
Rabies is the viral infection, which affects the central nervous system
of most mammals including humans; it is invariably fatal. However,
mammalian species vary widely in their susceptibility. Most clinical
cases are due to infection with rabies virus (genotype 1). A number of
other neurotropic lyssaviruses, closely related to the rabies virus,
produce clinical signs indistinguishable from rabies.
C.2. Etiology
Rhabdoviridae have characteristic rod shapes, a linear, non-
segmented, single-stranded RNA genome.
In a given geographical region, rabies is usually maintained and
transmitted by particular mammalian reservoir hosts.
More than 95% of human cases are the result of bites from rabid
dogs. Racoons, skunks, foxes and bats are important reservoirs of
rabies virus in North America. In continental Europe, the principal
reservoir is the red fox.
In developed countries, the control of stray dogs and the use of
vaccination programmes have reduced the importance of urban rabies
and have focused attention on wildlife reservoirs.
