❖ Control
The disease is reportable by law in many countries from which it has been eradicated by slaughter
of all infected and exposed animals. In countries where cattle movement can readily be restricted,
the disease can be eradicated by quarantine, blood testing, and slaughter. Where cattle cannot be

T1/44 strain). However, the vaccine is effective only if herd coverage within a country is high.
Treatment is recommended only in endemic areas because the organisms may not be eliminated,
and carriers may develop. Tylosin (10 mg/kg, IM, bid for 6 injections) and danofloxacin 2.5% (2.5
mg/kg, sid for 3consecutive days) have been reported to be effective.
2) Contagious Caprine Pleuropneumonia
Mycoplasma capricolum capripneumoniae (Mycoplasma biotype F38) is the causative agent. It
appears to be transmitted by infective aerosol. Pneumonia and pleuropneumonia can be caused by
other mycoplasmas, including M mycoides capri.
❖ Clinical Findings
Weakness, anorexia, cough, hyperpnea, and nasal discharge accompanied by fever (104.5°–106°F
[40.5°–41.5°C]) are often found. Exercise intolerance progresses to respiratory distress, with open-
mouth breathing and frothy salivation. A septicemic form of the disease without specific
respiratory tract involvement has been described.
