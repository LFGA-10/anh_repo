5.1.3.8. Black quarter
Blackleg, black quarter, quarter evil, quarter ill (Latin: Gangraena emphysematosa) is an
infectious bacterial disease most commonly caused by Clostridium chauvoei, a Gram-positive
bacterial species. It is seen in livestock all over the world, usually affecting cattle, sheep and goats.
❖ Clinical presentations
When infection begins, the animal may develop a fever, and the affected limb can feel hot to the
touch. The limb usually swells significantly, and the animal can develop lameness on the affected
leg. Crepitation (the sensation of air under the skin) can be noticed in many infections, as the area
seems to crackle under pressure.
