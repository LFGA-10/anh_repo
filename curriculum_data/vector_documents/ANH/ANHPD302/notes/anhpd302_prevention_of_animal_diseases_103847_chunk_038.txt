4.1. Acarians of veterinary interest
4.1.1 Generalities on acarians
Acarology is a biological area which covers mites and ticks. Although many have a veterinary
importance either directly or indirectly, there are many others which don’t. Many people have
tendency to confuse acarian (tick) with an insect but there is a significant difference. The table
below contrasts an acarian from an insect.
4.1.2 Ticks of Veterinary importance
Ticks are a relatively small group of large (2-30 mm in length), obligatoryhaematophage mites
that are of paramount importance as vectors of disease among domestic animals and are second
only to mosquitoes as vectors of human disease.
4.1.2.1 Classification of ticks
Within the Parasitiformes, ticks belong to the suborder Ixodida, which contains a single
superfamily, the Ixodoidea, that comprises two major families, Argasidae and Ixodidae, and the
rare family Nuttalliellidae, with a single African species.
✓ Ixodidae
These have a hard covering which may have a uniform colour or may have many colours which
also called hard ticks or Ixodid ticks.
