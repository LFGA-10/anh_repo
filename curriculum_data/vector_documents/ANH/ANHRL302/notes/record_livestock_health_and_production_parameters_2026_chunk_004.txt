Proper Steps to Livestock Tagging
3 | P a g e

1. Properly secure the animal to apply tag. Movement of the animal’s head could create an
undesirable situation to appropriately apply the ear tag. This could cause injury to the
person or livestock, or improper tagging of the animal. The following immobilization
suggestions work best, depending on species:
a. Cattle: Chute with a head gate, halter and/or nose lead.
b. Swine: Confine in a small pen, hog boards to restrict movement, or use of a
hog snare to completely secure the hog.
c. Sheep & Goats: Proper hand grip of animal’s head to restrict movement.
2. Identify the tagging site on the animal’s ear.
a. Tags should be applied in the middle third of the ear between the upper and lower
ribs
b. It is important, when using electronic identification (EID) tags to apply the tag
with the visual panel, male portion, of the tag on the outside back of the ear with
the EID button, female portion, of the tag on the inside of the ear.
3. Proper hygiene and cleanliness during the tagging process is necessary to reduce the risk
of infection. Use rubbing alcohol or a disinfectant solution to clean the jaw of the
applicator. Also, clean the tagging site of the animal’s ear.
4. Place each half of the tag onto the applicator. The stud must be inserted completely onto
the applicator pin and the panel/button portion placed under the opposite clip
5. Before tagging the animal, check alignment of the tagger by closing the jaw of the
applicator to the point where the two halves meet; the stud should be centered with the
hole
