2. Design a simple step-by-step plan for a small-scale farmer to make high-quality legume silage. Your plan should include considerations for harvesting timing, necessary materials, and the use of one specific additive, explaining its role. (10 Marks): 

text

**Model Answer:**

**Plan for Making Legume Silage (e.g., Lucerne):**

1.  **Harvesting Timing:** Harvest the lucerne at the **flower initiation stage** or when crown buds begin to grow. This is when protein content and sugars are high, and dry matter is optimal.

2.  **Wilting:** After cutting, allow the lucerne to wilt in the sun for a few hours. This is crucial for legumes to reduce the moisture content from around 80% to about 70%, preventing a too-wet silage that can lead to poor fermentation.

3.  **Chopping:** Use a **machete** or **chaff cutter** to chop the wilted lucerne into short pieces (1-2 inches). This helps in packing and excludes air.

4.  **Packing and Additive:**

    *   **Material:** Use a **barrel** or a pit lined with **polyethylene sheeting** as your silo.

    *   **Additive Application:** As you fill the barrel with layers of chopped lucerne, **dilute molasses** (1 part molasses to 3 parts water) and sprinkle it evenly over each layer using a garden sprayer.

    *   **Role of Molasses:** Molasses is a rich source of sugars. Legumes like lucerne are often low in fermentable sugars. Adding molasses provides the necessary food for the lactic acid bacteria, stimulating rapid fermentation, lowering the pH quickly, and producing more lactic acid, which preserves the silage effectively.
