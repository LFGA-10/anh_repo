Qn 5. Answer the following questions concisely.

(35 Marks)

1.  A farmer has a large quantity of wheat straw. List TWO physical and ONE chemical treatment they can use to improve its quality as livestock feed. (6 Marks)
* Physical: Grinding, Pelleting, or Soaking.
* Chemical: Treatment with Urea/Ammonia or Anhydrous Ammonia.

2. Compare and contrast homofermentative and heterofermentative silage inoculants in terms of their primary goal and main product. (8 Marks)
* Homofermentative: Primary goal is to rapidly lower pH and improve feeding quality by efficiently producing lactic acid. This reduces dry matter losses and preserves protein.
* Heterofermentative: Primary goal is to improve aerobic stability (reduce spoilage when exposed to air) by producing acetic acid (and some lactic acid), which is a better inhibitor of yeasts and molds.

3. List FIVE essential selection criteria for choosing materials for fodder conservation. (5 Marks)
* Conservation method (silage vs. hay)
* Size/quantity of fodder
* Cost
* Availability
* Efficiency

4.  Explain why the growth stage of a fodder crop is critical for both its nutritive value and its digestibility. (8 Marks)
* Nutritive Value: At peak vegetative growth (before maturity), plant sugars, proteins, and other nutrients are at their highest concentration.
* Digestibility: As the plant matures, the lignin content increases. Lignin is indigestible and forms strong bonds with cellulose and hemicellulose, making the entire plant fiber less digestible. Therefore, early-cut fodder is more digestible than late-cut.
