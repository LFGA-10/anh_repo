Heterofermentative (e.g., L. buchneri):

Goal: Improve aerobic stability at feed-out (prevent heating when silo is opened).

Product: Lactic acid, acetic acid, ethanol, CO2.

Best for: Silage that will be exposed to air during feeding. A farmer would choose this if they have a history of their silage heating or spoiling after opening the bunker.

Combination: A farmer might use a combined inoculant to get the fast initial pH drop from the homofermentative bacteria and the good aerobic stability later from the heterofermentative bacteria, offering the best of both worlds.

Evaluation Essay:

Advantages: Improve fermentation process, reduce nutrient losses, improve aerobic stability (less spoilage), increase nutritive value, lead to higher animal production and return on investment.

Disadvantages: Some are toxic/corrosive (safety risk), some have a bad smell, they add extra cost, and they can fail due to factors like crop type, dry matter, or native bacteria.

Proposed Guidelines for Farmers:

Assess Need: First, check if your silage often has problems (e.g., poor fermentation, heating at feed-out). If it's consistently good, an additive may not be necessary.

Identify the Problem: Choose an additive that targets your specific issue (e.g., homofermentative for better preservation, heterofermentative for stability, molasses for low-sugar crops).

Consider Crop and Conditions: Check the dry matter and sugar content of your crop. Be aware that additives are less effective on very dry or very wet forages and on crops like alfalfa which are naturally challenging.
