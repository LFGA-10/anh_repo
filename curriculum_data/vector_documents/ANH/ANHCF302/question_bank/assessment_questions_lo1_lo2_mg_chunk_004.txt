Level 4: Analyzing

A. Multiple Choice (Seeing relationships and patterns)

What is the key differentiating factor between the "wet" and "dry" methods of fodder conservation?
a) The type of fodder used
b) The moisture content at which the fodder is stored
c) The cost of implementation
d) The nutritional value of the final product
Answer: b) The moisture content at which the fodder is stored

Analyzing the problems listed for feeding crop residues, which problem is most directly linked to the physical form of the residue rather than its chemical composition?
a) Prussic acid poisoning from young sorghum shoots
b) Bloat from certain crops
c) Livestock choking on tubers or maize cobs
d) Iodine deficiency from brassicas
Answer: c) Livestock choking on tubers or maize cobs

B. Short Answer

Compare and contrast the primary goals of using homofermentative versus heterofermentative inoculants in silage production.
Answer: Homofermentative inoculants primarily aim for efficient fermentation, rapidly lowering pH to preserve nutrients and reduce dry matter losses. Heterofermentative inoculants primarily aim to improve aerobic stability at feedout by producing acetic acid, which inhibits yeast and mold, thus preventing heating when the silo is opened.

Why might a ruminant animal digest a high-fibre roughage better than a non-ruminant animal?
Answer: Ruminants have a pre-gastric fermentation chamber (the rumen) containing microbes that can break down fibrous materials like cellulose, which non-ruminants lack.
