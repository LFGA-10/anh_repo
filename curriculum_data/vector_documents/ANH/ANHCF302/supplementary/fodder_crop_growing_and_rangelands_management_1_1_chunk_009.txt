1.3 Fodder crops cultivation

Forage crops can be an important tool for farmers and   carefully managed during establishment and grazing ensures that the maximum productivity will be achieved.

1.3.1 Preparation of  land for fodder crop cultivation

One method of overcoming the problems caused by variations in yield and quality of feed due to the seasonal rainfall pattern is the use of fodder crops. These may be grown for grazing or for conservation as hay or silage.

In order to avoid repetitions, generals’ principals of fodder crop cultivation will be detailed before the particularities of some legumes and grasses.

Several requirements must be met in order to successfully fodder cultivation. These requirements are briefly discussed in the following sections.

1.3.1.1 Requirement of a site for fodder crop cultivation

The specific agronomic requirements for growing of forages differ from species to species and may be site specific. The following are factors that make particular place suitable for specific fodder crop:

1.Altitude

Some fodder crop better suited for low altitudes, while others prefer high altitudes

2.Temperature

Temperature has a very strong impact on crop growth, because it affects many physiological plant activities such as cell division, tissue growth, photosynthesis, germination, flowering, ripening, etc.
