4.3.3.3 Trifolium repens

This perennial plant is about 6" tall, branching from the base. Initially, it produces several compound leaves from a short stem that grows only a little, after which this stem rapidly elongates and becomes up to 1' long.

These elongated stems sprawl along the ground and have the capacity to root at the nodes.

They are hairless and light green. The alternate compound leaves are trifoliate and hairless.

 They occur at intervals along the elongated stems and have long hairless petioles. The leaflets are obovate or ovate.

Their margins are finely serrate. Across the upper surface of each leaflet are white markings in the form of a chevron (an upside down "V"), although for this species these markings are often degenerate, irregular, or absent. Each leaflet is about ¾" long and about half as wide.

At the base of each petiole there are pair of small lanceolate stipules that are light green and membranous; sometimes they wrap around the elongated stems. Each stipule is less than ½" in length. 

4.4 Improvement of natural pastures

Generally there are 3 essential activities to be done for improving natural pastures:

Bush and weed control

Provision of water

Over sowing/Reseeding

4.4.1 Bush and weed control

Bushes and weeds reduce grass productivity while some are poisonous. Food reserves in the root systems are exhausted by frequent uprooting and slashing, leading to their stunted growth and eventually death.
