Growth stage of fodder crops: Early cut fodder has higher digestibility than late-cut. With maturity lignin contents of plant/forage increases as digestibility is decreased.

Composition of fodder crops:  The chemical composition of feed or animal foods is one the biggest factors in deciding digestibility. This is because, as noted in the International Journal of Agriculture & Biology, the chemical composition of any animal food can contain many different quantities of materials that may reduce the efficacy of digestive enzymes. On the other hand, an ideal chemical composition can improve digestibility by the addition of soluble proteins and/or carbohydrates, such as isolated proteins.

Animal species: Roughages (portion of plant foods that the body can’t digest) high in crude fibre are better digested by ruminants than by non-ruminants due to the pre-gastric fermentative digestion that occurs in the ruminants.

Age of animal: Animal age directly influences their relationship to, and their capacity to digest, certain foods. Particularly old or young animals have particular needs in relation to food texture and nutritional composition, since the digestive system works differently at these stages.To improve digestive efficiency in animal nutrition, specific ingredients should ideally be chosen for formulations intended for adults and young, with the perpetual aim of improving nutrient absorption and assuring high palatability.

L U3: PERFORM SILAGE MAKING

Silage making is a method of preserving forage through wet method through anaerobic lactic fermentation. The stopping of the forage degradation is got through the combination of the lack of oxygen and a sufficient acidity, which blocks the activity of various bacteria and fungi.
