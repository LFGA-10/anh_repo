1.3.7.1 Desmodium

This legume has long creeping stems and is useful in mixtures with grasses at altitudes of 1200-1800m. The root nodule bacteria are effective at nitrogen fixation and the legume is therefore a valuable means of improving the quality of feed and the fertility of the land. However it does not withstand heavy grazing.

Desmodium can be grown as a sole crop or as an intercrop with grasses.

Planting methods

There are two common method of establishing Desmodium. These are:

from seeds

From cuttings.

Establishment from seeds

Desmodium requires a fine, firm and weed free seedbed.

Seedbed preparation should be done well before the onset of rain.

Use 3 - 5 kg Desmodium seeds/ha. (2kg/acre).

During planting, mix seed with 2 bags of TSP or 4 bags of SSP fertilizer. Farmers can also use 5 - 10 tons per hectare of well decomposed farm yard manure.

Drill the seed into shallow furrows of 2.5 deep spaced at 30 cm or 50 cm and then cover with little soil.

 For inter-cropping with napier grass, make furrows between or along the napier rows and drill Desmodium seeds/fertilizer mixture in the furrows.

Establishment from cuttings

Use mature parts of Desmodium vines.

 Use freshly cut Desmodium vines.
