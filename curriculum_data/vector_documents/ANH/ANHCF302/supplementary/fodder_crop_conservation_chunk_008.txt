Ammonia and Urea

Anhydrous ammonia or water or molasses-ammonia mixes have been used as silage additives. Ammonia additions have resulted in addition of an economical source of crude protein, prolonged bunk life during feeding (aerobic stability, less molding and heating during ensiling, decreased protein degradation in the silo.

Application of anhydrous ammonia should be at approximately 6 to 7 lb of N per 700 lb of forage DM.

Why Do Inoculants Not Work Sometimes?

There are several potential reasons why these products sometimes fail:

Natural bacteria population. It was mentioned in the introduction that natural lactic acid bacteria populations differ among crops. Generally, corn and sorghum have greater native bacteria populations than alfalfa.

Low sugar content of the crop. Water-soluble carbohydrates are the main food source for lactic acid bacteria. Crops with low sugars, like alfalfa and warm-season grasses, are more challenging for lactic acid bacteria to reduce pH and achieve good fermentation. It has been suggested that in crops like alfalfa, sugar content may limit the effect of inoculants

Dry matter content. Lactic acid bacteria do not move; they need water to move and intake sugars. Therefore, high dry matter content and low moisture will affect the activity of both the natural lactic acid bacteria and the inoculants. In contrast, very wet plant material will promote native bacteria to grow, affecting the impact of the inoculants on fermentation.
