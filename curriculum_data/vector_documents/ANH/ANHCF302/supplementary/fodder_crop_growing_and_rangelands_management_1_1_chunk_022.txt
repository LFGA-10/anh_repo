chemical control –pesticides

Chemical pest control involves the dusting, spraying of fumigation of a plant  with a substances specifically harmful to the particular pest, or ‘targeted organism’. This method has the advantages of speed of action, and it guarantees some degree of success.

Unfortunately, organism other than the target organism my be affected, with far-reaching consequence for the environment. Certain insecticides, the organochlorines, such as DDT are perisistent substances which remain indefinitely in the soil, water and in food crops. Some pesticides are toxic to man, and therefore great care must be exercised in their use. Fot these reasons pesticides should only be used in the event of failure of cultural control.

Biological control

Biological control involves the use of living agent or agent for the control of the pest. Thus the agent can be insect, virus or bacterium. The control agent can be either be introduced into an area or its number increases by creating an environment conducive to the multiplication of the agent. The control agent either controls the pest by feeding on it.
