4.Rainfall

Plants cannot survive without water and, therefore the availability of rain, being a natural source, as well as its distribution and reliability determines the nature and types of fodder crops in any given area. Different fodder crops have different water requirement ranges. Fodder crops that require a little amount of water in their life cycle are more suitable for arid and semi-arides areas, while those that need plenty of water can only be grown in regions which receive a reasonably well distributed rainfall through the year. Water should be enough for the fodder crop growing season since any shortage is bound to cause fodder crop to wilt and dry up; leads to or necessitate irrigation to keep them alive and healthy. Yet too much water is not good for plants either, as it limits the availability of air in the soil.

5.Sunlight

All plants require light from the sun for photosynthesis during their period. The amount of sunlight received in particular place affects the rate of this process because any increase in light intensity results in a corresponding increase in the photosynthesis. Some fodder crops need longer hours of sunlight than others to affecting the plant performance in relation to photoperiodism. Shortages of sufficient light lead to etiolated growth in plants, while too much sunlight and heat in the early stage of plant growth are equally harmful.
