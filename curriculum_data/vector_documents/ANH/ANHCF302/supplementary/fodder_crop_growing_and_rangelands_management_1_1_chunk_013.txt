Seedbed preparation operations loosen up the soil thereby facilitating rainfall infiltration into the soil as well as improving soil aeration. A well aerated promotes rapid root growth as well as increasing final yields.

Seedbed preparation may be aimed at breaking hard soil surface that may sometimes form with the result that rainfall acceptance of the soil is impeded. Seedbed preparation operations are sometimes directed at breaking the plough pan which may be impervious to water thereby limiting deep drainage.

There are two main operations involved in seedbed preparations. These are:

Primary operations

Secondary operations

Primary operations include activities as initial cleaning of land by cutting down the bush, or uprooting trees and tree-stumps and initial ploughing.

Secondary operations follow the primary operations and include mainly harrowing. Of necessity primary operations are a heavier job and therefore employ heavier implements as well as being more time consuming and costly. Secondary operations are less onerous and the rate of work is comparatively faster than for primary operations.
