1.2 Identification of fodder crops

Fodder crops are high yielding, high quality crops grown specifically for providing feed in intensive livestock grazing systems. These fodder crops are to be cut-and-carried to the animals. The technology of fodder crops and their use has had a very good impact with both zero-grazers and open-grazers. Natural pasture is a forage but is not grown as a crop, so is termed forage, not a forage crop. Forage crops produce much higher yields than natural forages and because they produce high yields.

Fodder crops are important because:

Pastures and fodder crops are the cheapest form of animal feed available (in term of quantity and quality). A constant supply of good quality forage in sufficient quantities is a basic necessity in livestock farming.

They provide a way of introducing farmers to the concept of improved livestock nutrition

They provide a large quantity of high quality and palatable fodder within a short time.

They are easy to grow; the husbandry requirements are similar to other crops that are familiar to the farmer.

Pastures and fodder, particularly the leguminous types, improve soil fertility through their ability to fix nitrogen.

If well managed, planted pastures help control soil erosion.

 Pasture seed production and sale of excess fodder can be a good source of income.

The major problems and limiting factors causing low fodder production are as follows:

small land area under fodder crops cultivation

cultivation of low yielding fodder crops

common use of traditional practices

lack of research based knowledge on fodder production and management
