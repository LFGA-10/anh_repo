Mix the seeds with sawdust, rough sand or phosphate fertilizer for even distribution. If the seed is mixed with fertilizer, plant immediately to prevent the fertilizer from scorching the seed.

 Grass seeds are most effectively sown with a wheat drill. Hand sowing is recommended for small acreages.

Immediately after sowing compact the seedbed to enhance germination of the grass seed by improving contact with the soil. Use tree branches or trample by foot on small plots. In mechanized farms, use a roller.

Undersowing

This is sowing of grasses and legumes into crops of maize, sorghum and millet (also known as nurse companion crops). Undersowing of Chloris Guyana into maize or sorghum has been successful in Rwanda. This would be a useful method especially since many farmers are unwilling to clear land for pasture establishment but will clear land for crops. The cost o pasture establishment will be absorbed in the cost of land preparation for crops. Sowing crops and pasture together has the advantage of suppressing weeds in the inter-row spaces and reducing soil erosion. Shade-tolerant and trailing legumes like Siratro, Centrosema, silver leaf Desmodium, Lablab establish well when sown after first weeding. Also stoloniferous grasses like Chloris and Bracharia establish well.

For successful undersowing observe the following:

Broadcast or drill pasture seed mixed with fertilizer 3 days after planting wheat or barley.

 Where maize is the nurse crop, broadcast pasture seed mixed with phosphate fertilizer in the maize field after the second weeding of maize (4 to 5 weeks after planting maize) or when maize is knee high.

After the nurse crop is harvested remove stovers or straws and cut back the weeds using a panga or slasher.
