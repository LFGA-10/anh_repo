For a flat surface:

1. Surface silo: are the piles of hay that are placed on a flat soil and which consist of packingforage on the ground and covering it with plastic sheeting without any borders, used for asmall herd. The advantage of that silo is that it is cheaper, it does not require any investment for the construction of edges and can be easily expanded, but one its disadvantages is that the silage quality is lower (packing difficult to do);

2. Pit silo: is a cylindrical hole made in the ground. Walls can be made of well packed or in bricks. The advantage of that silo is the low cost (only for digging), good compaction and thus the good silage quality, but the downside is that the silo unloading is bit easy.

B. For a sloping or flat ground:

Clamp silo: This silo consists of storing feed between two concrete walls, used for a high forage quantity. This system is more expensive because it requires the construction of walls, but the quality of products to preserve is better (the packing could be better done), and it is easy to unload silage.

For only a sloping ground:

Semi under-ground silo: it is a pit dug into the hillside. This system is ideal for slopes, and allows getting a good compaction and thus a good forage quality, and it is easy to remove silage.

Apart different types of silo discussed above silage can also be made in large plastic sacks or tubes. The plastic must have no holes to ensure no air enters. This is popularly referred to as tube silage.
