2.1.5. Baling hay

Baling the hay allows more material to be stored in a given space. A good estimate of the amount stored makes feed budgeting easier. Baling can be manual or mechanized, manual baling being more economical for small-scale dairy farmers.

Manual hay baling is done using a baling box with dimensions 85 cm long x 55 cm wide x 45 cm deep, open on both ends. If the hay is well pressed, the box will produce an average bale of 20 kg.

Hay can also be stored without baling by heaping it into a dome-shaped stack and covering it with a polythene sheet or a tarpaulin.

2.1.6 Hay storage

Large thunderstorms prevent hay from being properly preserved; it is then desirable to store it in bulk and pressed under a shed. But if it is harvested too wet, it will produce fermentations which will warm the mass and will make hay not much palatable and poor.

Hay can be preserved for long periods keeping intact its energy, proteins and minerals. Rats and other rodents should be controlled as they can damage the hay.

2.1.7. Characteristics of good-quality hay

The quality of the hay should be evident on physical examination. Good-quality hay should:

be leafy and greenish in color

have no foreign material mixed with it

have no smell

2.2 Silage making

Silage is a method of preserving forage through wet method (as opposed to hay drying or preservation by dry route) through anaerobic lactic fermentation. The stopping of forage degradation is got through the combination of the lack of oxygen and a sufficient acidity, which blocks the activity of various bacteria and fungi.
