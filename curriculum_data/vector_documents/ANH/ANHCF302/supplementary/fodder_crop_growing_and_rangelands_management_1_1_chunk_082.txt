Ranch management

In addition to rotation grazing and short season grazing there are two other ways of keeping the range land productive with adequate forage for grazing. It can be achieved by controlled grazing, controlled stocking and reseeding.

Deferred grazing

Here one paddock or area of the range land is left ungrazed every year. This allows seeds to develop, drop and germinate, thus maintaining the botanical composition of the pasture. Ticks decrease in number if cattle are absent for four months.

Continuous grazing

The stocking rate is one animal to between two and five hectares and the land is grazed continuously. This is suitable where large areas of land are available. Continuous grazing has disadvantages in that all nutritive species of pasture disappear with time due to selective grazing, and animals generally do not meet their nutritional requirements. There may also be a build up of internal worms.

Distribution of animals

Even distribution of animals on the ranch is important to avoid overgrazing certain areas. It is achieved by fencing, both perimeter and internal division fences, or by herding, which however is expensive. Distribution of animal can also be achieved by providing water at various points rather than having only one where animals crowd.

Water supply should be adequate, reliable and clean. It may be obtained by construction of earth dams, tapping natural streams, sinking wells, or use of natural springs.
