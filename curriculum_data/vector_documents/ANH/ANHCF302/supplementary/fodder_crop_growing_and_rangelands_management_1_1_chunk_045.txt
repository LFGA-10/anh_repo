We must harvest forage when their moisture content is between 60 and 72%. To reach this rate, we operate, after cutting grasses, a first drying of 3 to 4 hours to increase the rate of drymatter (35%).

For making a good silage, we must prevent the growth of mold and some bacteria by making anaerobic and very acidic conditions (pH<4). This can be done by creating favorable conditions for the development of lactic bacteria, while preventing the development of butyric bacteria (pH> 5).

Thus, the grass must be sufficiently rich in soluble carbohydrates and low in nitrogen tofurther lactic and acetic bacteria, to rapidly decrease the pH. Furthermore, we shall avoid the introduction of soil in the silo.

We must ensure that the grasses are well compacted enough in order to remove oxygen fromthe grass. Thus, the grass will be first cut into small pieces of 3 to 5 cm long in order to facilitate the compaction.

The success of the silage of tropical forages can be helped by the addition of molasses (4kg/100 kg of green forage) because molasses will serve as feed easy for lactic bacteria, leadingrapidly to reach the required acidity.

For very wet grasses, some conservatives like formic acid, in combination or not with formalin,can also help to maintain a low pH while inhibiting butyric bacteria. However, the quantity ofcarbohydrates must be initially sufficient. Also, their cost remains high, making their use little common.
