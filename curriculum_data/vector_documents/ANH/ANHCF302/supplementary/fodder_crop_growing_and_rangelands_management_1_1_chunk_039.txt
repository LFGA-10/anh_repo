Depending on various techniques used, we get a fodder with a pH below 4 whose the moisture content varies from 50% to 85%. Generally, the higher the dry matter, the more difficult is to get the required anaerobiosis to start lactic fermentation.

Preservation through wet method called “silage”, is applied both to forage grasses and to maize and possibly to agri-food byproducts as pulp, dried spent grains, etc. However, we should ensure that the grass to ensile is rich in sugar and relatively low in nitrogen. It is thus difficult to achieve with some forages such as alfalfa, low in sugar and high in soluble nitrogen.

2.2.1 Advantages of silage making

Through silage, we get stored forage that keeps its nutritive qualities. Silage can also be easily made in the wet season because the grass should not be dried.

2.2.2 Disadvantages of silage making

Silage making is a method that can cause toxicity risks when certain precautions are not taken.

Risk of botulism: it is related to the possible presence of dead animals in plant bales or in the silo.

Risk of intoxication caused by fungal or bacterial toxins fungal: It is a risk for animals that consume the products of poorly made silage (e.g. aflatoxins).
