Acids and acid salts

To prevent bad quality silage, mixes of formic and propionic acid and their salts are used to improve the ensilage process. Good silage needs a fermentation that is in an anaerobic environment, reaching the right pH and thereby saving nutritional value and dry matter content. Our acids and their salts help that process in the right direction for our customers. Examples of those acids are Formic acid which decreases pH, saves nutritional value and kills unwanted bacteria and Propionic acid which inhibits mold and yeast growth and prevents heating processes and Sodium formate and sodium propionate which is effective against unwanted bacteria such as Clostridium.

Molasses and sugars.

These molasses and sugars affects forages in the following ways:

Stimulate fermentation and facilitates natural silage preservation

Increases lactic acid production, lowers Ammonia-N and silage pH

Improves dry matter digestibility (DMD) and Silage intake

Rich source of natural sugar and energy

Molasses dilution

Dilute 1 litre of molasses with 3 litres of water. Sprinkle this mixture over the layer of chopped forage. Use a garden sprayer to distribute the solution evenly. This helps to feed the micro-organisms to mak, the silage acid quickly, which will prevent rotting.

Problems associated with too much molasses feeding

Three metabolic diseases may occur in cattle and sheep fed diets in which molasses is used as a supplement (as a vehicle for urea) or as the basis of the diet. These are: urea toxicity, molasses toxicity and bloat.
