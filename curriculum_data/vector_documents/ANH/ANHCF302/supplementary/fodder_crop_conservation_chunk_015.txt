Semi underground silo:

You horizontally dig a hole on a medium sloping site (30%)

The usual dimensions are 2 m high and 2 m wide for 6 m long but this depends strongly on the site.

NB: to maximize the quality of silage, we should also isolate the sides with a material impermeable to air and water such as plastic or bricks and the whole silage is covered with a waterproof and hermetic tarp.

Clamp silo:

Construct three concrete walls and a concrete creed

This is usually used to store large quantities of fodder

The most common dimensions are 2.40 m high, 3 m wide, the length is variable but at least 6 m. the volume is then about 20 m3.

Surface silo:

It is built by heaping up forage on more or less horizontal site and plastic tarp coverage

Dimensions are very variable

It is a commonly used type of silo and which can be adapted to Rwanda because it is cheaper compared with others bur it represents a disadvantage of pressing that is not good.

Types of fermentation which take place within the silo

Acetic fermentation: the silo is initially colonized by aerobic acetic bacteria that early start and continue to grow if there is still oxygen. They convert soluble sugars in acetic and lactic acid, alcohol and carbon dioxide. These bacteria rapidly disappear when the environment becomes too acidic (PH less than 4.5) and in the absence of oxygen).

Lactic fermentation: then in second stage appear lactic bacteria that produce only lactic acid. The silo quickly becomes acidified (PH drops below 4) blocking any other fermentation. The lactic fermentation occurs in anaerobic conditions.

Butyric fermentation: it converts the lactic acid into butyric acid, amino acids into ammonia and fatty acids or into amines and some are toxic.
