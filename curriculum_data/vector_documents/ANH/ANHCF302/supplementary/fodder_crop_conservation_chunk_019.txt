ting is the process where forage is left in the field to dry down usually in windrows to raise the dry matter level in the crop prior to being chopped and ensiled.

3.4.1. Objectives of fodder Wilting

Is wilting grass silage really important? Wilting is an important step when making grass silage as:

It increases the dry matter (DM) percentage and

reduces the quantity of effluent produced.

It reduces transportation costs by removing surplus water

Factors that affect wilting rates

Climatic factors such as: temperature, relative humidity, wind, rain, dew, soil moisture, and solar radiation. 

Crop factors such as: character of the plant

Husbandry factors such as: distribution of the forage in the field, times of turning over

3.4.2.  Techniques of fodder Wilting

Mow lighter crops (i.e. earlier growth stage);

Use a mower-conditioner;

Leave the band of the conditioned forage as wide as possible (moisture evaporates relatively quickly from thin, wide bands);

Spread the band with a tedder or tedder-rake for more effective drying by the sun and wind.

3.5: CHOP FODDER CROPS FOR SILAGE MAKING

Chopping is a laborious and time consuming process which is to cut plant matter or crops by either hand or recommended machines to feed to livestock.

Objectives of forages chopping before feeding to animals

In summary precision chopping of forages:

Help the rumen bacteria process the diet by opening up the high fibre materials

Speeds up the rate at which the diet leaves the rumen increasing the amount of food the animal can eat

Homogenizes the plant material in the windrow thoroughly mixing small and large particles

Benefits medium and low quality forages most but allowing greater net daily intake of nutrients.
