3.2.7. Advantages of silage

It is wet forage that keeps its nutritive value or qualities.

Silage can be easily made in wet season when the forages are more.

It decreases the cost of feeds

It lowers harvest losses

It increases the forage quality

Silage also can reduce labor needs through greater mechanization of harvesting and feeding.

3.2.8. risk of silage

Risk of botulism: it is related to the possible presence of dead animals in plant bales or in the silo.

Risk of intoxication: this is caused by fungal or bacteria toxins. It is a risk for animals that consume the products of a poorly made silage. For example, aflatoxins.

Risk of listeria: in case of a poorly acidified silage which allows the bacteria development including in dairy livestock farming, especially when we produce cheese made from milk.

Risk of the production of ethanol which is toxic to ruminants, usually as a result of alcoholic fermentation due to the improper sealing of silo or the plastic sheeting.

Risk of the excessive presence of butyric bacteria, following the incorporation of soil in the forage during harvesting operations.

Risk of pollution: the liquid produced by the silage of too wet plants is acidic, corrosive, odorous and pollutant.

Requires equipment and labor

Needs to be prepared quickly and correctly

It can be spoil if not prepared correctly

3.4: fodder crops wilting

3.4.1: definition of fodder wilting

What does wilting mean? Wilting is the process where forage is left in the field to dry down usually in windrows to raise the dry matter level in the crop prior to being chopped and ensiled.
