1.3.6.2 Tripsacum laxum

Tripsacum laxum is a perennial, similar to Napier grass although it has smoother leaves without bristles and is much deeper in color, more like zones.

The roots are shallow and the plant does not grow well during a long dry season. As the grass matures, the roots become stronger and store nutrients that will be necessary for regrowth after cuttings.

The leaves are tall (0.4-1.2 m long x 9 cm broad), Flowers are mostly sterile.

Planting technique

Tripsacum laxum grow wellin fertile and well-drained soils but can tolerate low pH . It is grown from stem cuttings or from division of the root stock. Specing is 75cmx30cm or 90cmx60cm depending on the soil fertility and rainfall.

Management

The best age for cutting is 8-10 weeks and the ground so as to leave stumps from which the next crop will grow.

After each cutting, it is advisable to top dress with a nitrogenous fertilizer at a rate of 230kg/hectare to stimulate foliage production. An application of 200kg P2O5 per hectare is useful at planting time.

Researchers have recommended intercropping with Desmodium uncinatum so as to reduce the need for a nitrogen application and increase the protein content if being fed to livestock

With a good level of rainfall or irrigation, 110t/ha of Tripsacum laxum are obtained per hectare per cutting. About 27kg of the grass gives enough maintenance ration for a 45kg cow per day.
