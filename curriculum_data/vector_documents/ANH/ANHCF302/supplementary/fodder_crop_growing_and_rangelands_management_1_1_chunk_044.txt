Lactic fermentation

Then in a second stage appear lactic bacteria that produce only lactic acid. The silo quicklybecomes acidified (pH drops below 4) blocking any other fermentation. The lacticfermentation occurs in anaerobic conditions.

Butyric fermentation

The butyric fermentation converts the lactic acid into butyric acid, or the amino acids intoammonia and fatty acids, or into amines whose some are toxic.

This fermentation is developed in aerobic or anaerobic conditions but stops when the pH drops to 5. This fermentation results in the rapid deterioration of the silage that graduallyputrefies.

For making a good silage, we must prevent the growth of mold and some bacteria by makinganaerobic and very acidic conditions (pH<4). This can be done by creating favorable conditionsfor the development of lactic bacteria, while preventing the development of butyric bacteria(pH> 5).

2.2.6 Ensiling and unloading fodder

A. Grass for the silage

We must harvest forage when their moisture content is between 60 and 72%. To reach this rate, we operate, after cutting grasses, a first drying of 3 to 4 hours to increase the rate of drymatter (35%).
