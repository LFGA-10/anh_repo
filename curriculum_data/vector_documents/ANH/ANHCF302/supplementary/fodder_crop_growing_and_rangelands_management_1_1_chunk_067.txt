4.5 Pastures establishment

Generally there are 4 essential steps to be followed for establishing improved pastures:

Perimeter fencing

Paddocking

Establishment of watering points/troughs

Land preparation

Planting fodder

Successful pasture establishment is important to ensuring high levels of production and persistence in improved pastures.

4.5.1 Perimeter fencing

Perimeter fencing using barbed wire or live hedges will exclude unwanted animals from grazing the pasture and will help control grazing. Paddocking is also recommended where possible as this makes rotational grazing for efficient pasture utilization possible. Euphorbia tirucalli, Ficus tree and Erythrina abysinnica are trees mostly used for live hedges but any other appropriate trees can be used. The use of live hedges cuts down on the cost of purchasing fencing posts.

Fencing is important for:

Excluding non-farm animals’ access to the pastures.

Controlling grazing.

Allowing for paddocks hence rotational grazing.

Helping farmer to implement pasture improvement techniques.

Protection of fodder banks.
