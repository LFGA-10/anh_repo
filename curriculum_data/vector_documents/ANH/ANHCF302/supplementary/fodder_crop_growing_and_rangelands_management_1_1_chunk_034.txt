A well maintained and manuredCalliandra can continuously produce fodder for 10 – 20 years.

LeucaenaleucocephalaLeucaena is a long-lived shrub or tree up to 20 m high. It is a vigorous plant of high yield and high-quality protein; leaves and thin twigs well-accepted by livestock.

Leucaena—also does well in upland areas with medium to high rainfall. It is slightly more drought resistant but is more subject to attack by insect pests. Regrows well after cutting and harvesting.

Cajanus cajan

Pigeon pea is an erect shrub or short-lived perennial herb.  Adapted to arid and semi-arid environments, drought-resistant but susceptible to frost; can grow at 500–800 m annual rainfall; tolerates acid soils.Established from seed sown at the rate of 4–6 kg/ ha or 1–20 kg/ha broadcast. Spacing: about 1 m apart between rows. Seed scarification and inoculation are not necessary. Responds favourably to P fertilizer but negatively to N.

Fodder trees can be cultivated:

Along farm boundaries to leave all the available land for crop fields

Along roads, passages and terraces to make them stable and avoid soil erosion.

 Around the homestead to provide privacy

 In plots of Napier grass, so that they can be harvested together as a mixture.

Fodder trees cultivation techniques

Make a nursery for Calliandra and Leuceana seeds. Seeds are planted in lines at 5 cm distance between each other and 10 – 15 cm from seed toseed in the line

To improve germination of the seeds these should be soaked in cold water for 72 hours

Mulberry cuttings can be planted directly in the field during the rainy season. Pigeon pea should be sawn in November after the rains.
