Pasture can be defined as any plant community in which forages namely,grasses, legumes, herbs and trees form the dominant vegetation upon which domestic ruminant livestock depend for food.

Rwanda has six dominant types of natural grasslands, which are adapted to specific agro-ecological zones. This is mostly determined by the amount of rainfall and soil fertility. The management of natural pastures is an integral system involving management of the plants and soil on one hand and herd management the other hand.

There are two major types of pastures:

Natural

Artificial

4.1 Natural pasture

It is a pasture which has developed naturally under the ecological conditions of the prevailing environment conditions. Natural pasture is native or hard. The vegetation of natural pastures consists of wild perennial herbs, lichens (tundra), subshrubs, and shrubs

4.2 Artificial, or planted pasture

 It is a pasture that contains grasses that are tame, non-native or soft and tend to have a much higher forage biomass over a growing making them ideal for grazing. Artificial pastures contain grasses that are more vigorous, competitive in a controlled system over a nomadic pastoral system typical of natural pastures.

4.3. Common pasture fodder trees and shrubs

The following are examples of fodder trees and shrubs that are planted in artificial pasture

4.3. 1.1 Sesbania sesban

Sesbania sesban is a narrow-crowned, deep-rooting single or multi stemmed shrub or small tree, 1-7 m tall. The trees usually have a main stem but may develop many side branches if widely spaced.

 The many branches give the tree a shrubby appearance, often tending towards a spreading habit due to its wide branching angle (45-60 deg. Mostly).
