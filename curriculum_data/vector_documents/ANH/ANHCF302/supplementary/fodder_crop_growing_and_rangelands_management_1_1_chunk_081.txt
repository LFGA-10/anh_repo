5.3.4.6 Ranching

A rang or ranch is extensive grazing land usually covering several hundreds of hectares. The pasture is usually growing grass which may be improved by oversowing, fertilizer application, and weeding out undesirable shrubs and grass species.

The aims of management of ranching should be:

To decrease the annual fluctuations in stocking numbers

To decrease the seasonal fluctuations in live weight of stock

To maximize reproductive performance towards the ideal of one calf per cow per year

To maintain and improve feeding efficiency.

Basic facilities required

In establishing a ranch, there are some basic facilities that are required. These include, a kraal, shed water supply usually around the kraal, and sufficient land for grazing  and production of fodder crop and supplementary feed crop such as grain.

Size of the herd

The size of a herd varies between one hundred and several thousand heads of animals, depending on the following factors:

The type of pasture species, nutritive value and therefore carrying capacity

The productivity of the pasture species

The type of stock, for example, hardy types which can survive well in drier conditions and scarce pasture, can kept  in large numbers

Availability of reserve feeds, which enable  a large herd to be carried through dry periods when pasture is scarce

Economic considerations

Topography of the land, where over-stocking in hilly areas could result into a serious case of soil erosion

Availability of water, sufficient and available at all times
