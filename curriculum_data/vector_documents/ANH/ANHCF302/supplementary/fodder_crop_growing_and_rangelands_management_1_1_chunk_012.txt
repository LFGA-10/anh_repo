6.Humidity

Relative humidity experienced at any one place affects variety of plant life process because the availability of high or low humidity in the atmosphere is dependent on temperature. A high humidity lowers the rate of evaporation and transpiration and, consequently, water intake through the roots. Warm temperatures in a humid environment conduce to the rapid multiplication of disease causing germs and, therefore, are not good for susceptible plants.

1.3.1.2 Seedbed Preparation

A seedbed is a piece of land varying in size from a few square meter to hundred and even thousands of hectares, that has been prepared in such a way that it is ready to receive the seed or planting material. For the land to be ready for planting, the clods, depth of soil and the looseness of the soil must facilitate planting, germination of the seed and subsequent growth of the plants. Seedbed preparation operations include activities such as:

Ploughing or digging( if hand tools are used)

Harrowing to break large soil clods into smaller ones, and sometimes rolling of the ground to firm the soil

The main reasons for preparing a seedbed are as follows:

To kill the weeds either by burying them or by desiccation through exposures to the sun.

To bury crop residues from previous season’s crop so as to make it easy to plant. By burying the trash, decomposition of animal and vegetable matter is speeded up resulting in addition of organic matter into the soil as well as releasing of nutrients for use by the plants.
