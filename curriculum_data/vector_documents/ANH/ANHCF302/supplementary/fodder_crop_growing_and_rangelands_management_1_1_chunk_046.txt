Ensiling a silo

Every silo must be filled quickly following the steps bellow:

Chop the wilted material to be ensiled into pieces not more than 2.5 cm long

Sprinkle the chopped material with a molasses and water mixture; for every sack use 1 litre of molasses mixed with 2–3 times of water. This is especially for material like Napier grass that has low sugar content. Maize bran or cassava flour can be added to improve the carbohydrate (energy) content

Place the chopped material, sprinkled with the molasses and water mixture, into the silo, compressing it well. The compaction is usually the most neglected step in filling silos. A dense compaction reduces the losses of dry matter and heating problems and storage costs.

The silo must be filled generally in 1 to 2 days. When filled cover the top of the silo with a sheet of plastic and covering with the soil in order to keep out water and air. Filling the silo as quickly as possible, we minimize the exposure of the silage to air and rains.

When filled cover the top of the silo with a sheet of plastic or stones and a covering of soil in order to keep out water and air. Leave the silage for a few months before using it. By filling the silo as quickly as possible, we minimize the exposure of the silage to air and rains.
