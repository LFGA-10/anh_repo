3.3 Benefit of using crop residues

They can be an important source of animal feeds especially during the dry season

They work well when used for mulching fields

Legume crop residues have high protein and better feeding value than cereal residues.

3.4 Limitation of using crop residues

They are generally low in protein, energy, minerals and vitamins.

They have a high fiber content resulting in low digestibility and intake.

The can only provide enough nutrients for general body maintenance rather than for production. It is therefore necessary to supplement crop residues with planted fodder or concentrate feeds to improve their quality.

They are also bulky, making their transportation and storage difficult and in addition often require forage choppers for processing.

Some crop residues contain anti-nutritional factors like tannin and silca.

Lack of synchronized planting time and scattered small farms makes uniform harvesting difficult.

When they are used as feed, it is important to return manure to the field to restore the soil structure and add some nutrients for the next crop.

Learning unit 4: Pastures lay out and improvement

Pasture can be defined as any plant community in which forages namely,grasses, legumes, herbs and trees form the dominant vegetation upon which domestic ruminant livestock depend for food.
