1.5.3. Animal nutritional requirements
Nutrients are elements in feed that are used by the animals for growth and
production.
Nutrients are normally divided into five (5) categories:
➢ Water
➢ Protein

➢ Carbohydrates
➢ Minerals
➢ Vitamins
Rations should consist of sufficient protein, energy, roughages and minerals to
maintain the rumen function and support livestock growth to meet the market
specifications. Minerals of particular importance to livestock growth are calcium
and phosphorus and these may be supplied as individual components of a total
mixed ration.
Factors affecting animal nutritional requirements:
➢ Species:
➢ Breed
➢ Weight
➢ Body condition
➢ Age
➢ Sex
➢ Growth: for example, protein requirements will be higher for young
animals because they are gaining more muscles than fat.
➢ Stage of gestation: higher nutrient demands is at the third trimester where
the fetus is growing rapidly.
➢ Level of milk production: heavy lactation has greater nutrient demands
than any other production stage. animals that produce more milk and milk
with more fat and protein will have higher nutrient requirement.
➢ Work
➢ Environment
➢ Others (genetic potential, diseases, stress)
