✓ Heterofermentative inoculants: Heterofermentative bacteria, such
as Lactobacillus buchneri, produce lactic acid, acetic acid, ethanol, and

carbon dioxide. The main purpose of heterofermentative inoculants is to
improve aerobic (the presence of oxygen) stability by reducing the level of
yeast in the silage (high levels of yeast can cause heating). it is
recommended that a minimum of 45–60 days’ elapse before opening the
silo in order to ensure good aerobic stability.
NB: The potential advantages of combining both types of lactic acid
bacteria are having a fast initial pH reduction controlled by the
homofermentative bacteria and a good aerobic stability later that is
controlled by heterofermentative bacteria producing more acetic acid
✓ Acids and acid salts
To prevent bad quality silage, mixes of formic and propionic acid and their salts
are used to improve the ensilage process. Good silage needs a fermentation that
is in an anaerobic environment, reaching the right pH and thereby saving
nutritional value and dry matter content. Our acids and their salts help that
process in the right direction for our customers. Examples of those acids are
Formic acid which decreases pH, saves nutritional value and kills unwanted
bacteria and Propionic acid which inhibits mold and yeast growth and prevents
