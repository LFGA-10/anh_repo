4.8. Disadvantages of hay drying
✓ Loss of nutrient
✓ Decrease of digestibility
✓ Decrease of energy value and protein value decrease by 10 to 20%
✓ Decrease in vitamin A
Benefits of hay drying
✓ Increasing forage DM content
✓ It allows better valorization of forage when the nutritive value is very high
4.9. Methods of curing
Hay drying technique involves several steps:
1) Mowing: the plant should be cut at early flowering for legumes. Mowing
can be done using sickle when areas are small, or using tractor mounted
mowers when areas are very large.
2) Drying: it must be the most rapid possible and must be done in open fields
in the sun. the grass must be turned 2-3 times a day. During rainy period,
we must provide the maximum protection against water, by making small
ricks that will be scattered once the rain ceases. Hay is dry after 2 days of
good weather and contents about 78% of DM.
3) Storage: it must be stored under the shade. Hay can be preserved for long
periods keeping intact its energy, proteins and minerals.
