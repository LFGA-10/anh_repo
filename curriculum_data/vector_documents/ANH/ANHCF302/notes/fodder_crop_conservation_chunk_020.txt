3.4.2. Techniques of fodder Wilting
✓ Mow lighter crops (i.e. earlier growth stage);
✓ Use a mower-conditioner;
✓ Leave the band of the conditioned forage as wide as possible (moisture
evaporates relatively quickly from thin, wide bands);
✓ Spread the band with a tedder or tedder-rake for more effective drying by
the sun and wind.
3.5: CHOP FODDER CROPS FOR SILAGE MAKING
Chopping is a laborious and time consuming process which is to cut plant
matter or crops by either hand or recommended machines to feed to livestock.
1.5.1. Objectives of forages chopping before feeding to animals
In summary precision chopping of forages:
➢ Help the rumen bacteria process the diet by opening up the high
fibre materials
➢ Speeds up the rate at which the diet leaves the rumen increasing
the amount of food the animal can eat
➢ Homogenizes the plant material in the windrow thoroughly
mixing small and large particles
➢ Benefits medium and low quality forages most but allowing
greater net daily intake of nutrients.
3.5.1. FODDER CROPS CHOPPING TECHNIQUES
➢ Manual chopping: By using a machete or knife
➢ Mechanical chopping: By motor-driven choppers or Chaff cutter
1.5.2. The size of chopped fodder in length
Because good packing and exclusion of oxygen are so critical to silage
fermentation and minimize DM losses, it is good thought to chop
grasses at shorter lengths to aid compaction.
NB: 2 and 4 cm is desirable
